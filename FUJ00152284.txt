1 MANAGEMENT SUMMARY

Within the Fujitsu Services domain for Apr 08 there were no Major Incidents and 6 service-affecting incidents, 3
incident relating to a VIP site and no further service-affecting incidents outside the Fujitsu domain, detailed in
the incident overview below.

Branch and counter availability continue to remain above target at 99.80% and 99.56% respectively. The pilot of
the revised Engineering service has now reached its conclusion and has been viewed as a success by all
parties. These are now to be made contractual with underpinning KPI's to drive continual service improvement.
The engineering service has now stabilised and SLT achievement significantly improved.

The Pin Pad refresh button introduced in February and saw 46 incidents resolved at the helpdesk that would
otherwise have required an engineering visit, significantly reducing the downtime of the counter or single
counter branch.

2 offsite builds were completed allowing early recommencement of services to the affected branches that
otherwise would have to wait for a PSTN fault to be resolved by BT.

Preventative maintenance visits were commenced with 109 taken place to date. Only 20 of the sites have
subsequently logged a call. There have been no Back Office printer calls logged for any of the sites. The
expectation is that these visits will reduce the number of incidents and provide an enhanced service to the Post
Office.

342 OBC changes were delivered in the month, with 3 failures due to BT Line Plant issues, which were
unavoidable.

1 non critical incident failed the Post Shops SLT, with all critical incidents being achieved in the timescales.

The overall number of complaints logged with NBSC has decreased in April to 63, the lowest number for some
considerable time, with 2 considered to be justified. Some additional graphs have been added to this Service
Review Book to show trends and associated activities to improve in this area.

No branches failed the Day J targets.

Significant activities from the Service Improvement Plan are included in this review book. A number of the
branch improvements have been in pilot and demonstrated positive results, and these will be rolled out in
agreement with POL over the coming weeks.

during April.

5.2 Engineering Service

Call volumes during April increased to 4482 with the main increases attributed to Pin Pad and counter printer
calls. Sense & Respond actions are in place to break down the top failure demand areas. There were 606 A
priority calls during this period.

conformance figures. Whilst all Base Unit SLTs are below agreed targets, the reality is that one extra passed
call would have resulted in all SLTs passing.

the best conformance figures since the trial began were recorded.

One of the key messages taken from the Customer was the desire for us to deliver an enhanced service to WH
Smith branches. Whilst no contract is in place for this, we have worked on a reasonable endeavours basis to
provide a same day service for WH Smith and VIP branches. 25% of all VIP C priority calls logged were
attended the same day and 16% of all WH Smith calls logged were also attended on the day the call was

5.3. Branch Network Service / Central Network

5.3.1 Branch Network Service

During April 1508 calls were logged across all Network Types. This is a decrease of 78 calls from March. The
reduction of calls is in part due to the IP Max upgrade being completed.

In total 30 calls failed to hit the 4 hour priority SLA. This gave 98% SLT conformance against the 95% target;
this is an improvement on last months’ 40 failed calls and 97.5% conformance.

We have identified a small number of branches which have had intermittent faults since the IPMax upgrade,.
Together with Core and BT we are trialling a cap on the line rate; a form of re-grading the branch to its original
line settings. Three of the four sites tested have shown improvement. This has been trialled for only a short time,
but the end result is the resumption of perfect communications to the few sites affected by the IPMax upgrade.
Further results will be reported next month.

The combined efforts of Branch Network services, POL and the Communications Management Team have
reduced this months GSM bill. New scripts and new reporting is allowing both POL and Fujitsu Services to
efficiently monitor GSM usage and monitor the quantity of modems both out in the estate and in engineering
holding.

The Branch Network Services Troubleshooting Guide is currently in review and will be released to the helpdesk
and engineers once approved by all parties.

5.3.2 Central Network
While there were no major incidents this month there were several incidents causing disruption to the VSAT and
A&L connectivity.
The A&L connectivity problem affected multiple C&W IP customers and after Fujitsu escalation was resolved on
17th April. Several actions are ongoing in the C&W service review to improve their NMC diagnostic processes to

identify these types of IP core faults earlier.

BT has completed a corrective action to ensure that the Turin switch resilience is now working. The incident of
29" April affected all of BT's customers using VSAT Internet access through the Turin base station.

7 Sense & Respond

The data captured during April has highlighted the top 2 highest demand types as being Hardware and
Software. Trend analysis has shown the total call volume for Hardware during April to be 7788 and Software
calls to be 2315.

For Pin Pad errors, the volume of Pin Pad calls for April totalled at 1394 (an increase of 92 calls from March).
HSD frontline have been advised to check Tivoli events for all Pin Pad calls received to avoid any unnecessary
HSD diagnostics/engineer visits. It is thought that the additional calls are due to the new Pin Pad data table
refresh button, which was the resolution for 263 calls per month; agents have been advised to check if this fix
has already been attempted when a Pin Pad call is logged to avoid unnecessary additional incident logs being
raised for a single incident.

For hardware demand, the highest call volume received is for Counter Printer faults (2581 calls received in April
- a decrease of just 2 calls from March). The ongoing Monday morning call peaks suggest that a period of
inactivity may cause the ink inside the printer head to dry up: resolutions are being researched. 73% of incidents
are however currently resolved by the helpdesk.

During April, 1565 customer chase calls were received, a decrease of 186 calls (or 11%) from March. Ongoing
actions include agents advising PMs not to call for ETAs on C priority incidents until the SLA has passed: this
action had previously been closed but further analysis revealed a possible training need with some agents.

In regards to the project focusing on base unit calls, ‘Unit replacements’ are continuing to be the highest repair
reason for calls, followed by ‘user advise / no action taken.’ Call volumes from February to April has reduced,
but overall performance has been stable, with FMS resolving 84% of calls in February, 87% of calls in March
and 85% in April. ‘No action taken’ calls are being analysed to discover the root cause; this should allow a
reduction in call volume.

For the monitor faults project, 1030 calls were logged during April as a monitor fault (-164 calls from March).
This represented a total of 757 faults (73%) being resolved by engineering and 273 faults (27%) by the HSD. A
Sharing Knowledge meeting with Optima is being arranged, in order for HSD to identify further
checks/resolutions which can be performed by HSD.

After tracking Inappropriate calls data again throughout April, the calls received at the HSD totalled at 1683, an
increase on March. This represents 11% of all calls.

HSD has highlighted all the sites that have made 3 or more Inappropriate calls for the month of April and passed
this information direct to POL. Outgoing coaching calls to be provided by HSD for repeat offenders. The aim is
to identify any outstanding issues at those Post Offices and to also coach the PM's to understand the difference
between the Horizon and NBSC desks. A potential article for distribution in Ops Focus is being drafted and will
aim to reduce confusion.
