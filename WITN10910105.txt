Post Office

The Post Office Limited (POL — people will generally refer to it as this) is dealt with by UKGI. They are a separate
company to Royal Mail and do separate things — they provide the front of office service for post (in addition to a
number of other things).

The main points of contact in UKGI are:
Richard Callard — Deputy Director, Post Office Limited and GIB.
Michael Dollin — Assistant Director, Network Transformation (including Franchising) and Stakeholder Engagement

Laura Thompson — Assistant Director, Appointments and Remuneration, Horizon IT system c
Adam Shortman — Comms, Competitive Markets and Responsible Business}

Funding:
The current funding bid is in the process of being submitted to HMT. The budget runs for three years, starting 2018.
The current bid is higher than it has been in previous years. It is set into three parts:
e Network Subsidy — the Government committed to keeping open rural Post Offices if they were the ‘last shop
in the village’. Therefore we provide subsidy, as many of them are loss making.
e Network Investment — This is to provide a fund to improve the infrastructure of the network. This includes IT
Investment and Central costs.
© Commercial Loan Facility - POL may not necessarily use this, but want an additional fund available. HMG
have imposed the rule that they cannot borrow money in the usual way companies do, but as this is such a
large company they feel they should be able to access loan features.
This has been subject to a UKGI-commissioned KPMG report and is being finalised before it is approved by HMT.
There was a hope it would be approved by the March budget, but it looks as if it will continue into the year — for
Autumn Statement. However, it will need to be finalised as quickly as possible to ensure stability of the network.
Angie Ridgwell, DG of Finance, sent a letter of comfort to the POL Board regarding their funding bid. The funding
submission is attached.

Consultation:

Before Christmas UKGI carried out a Government Consultation to ask certain questions about the Post Office, what
it could do better etc. UKGI are currently reviewing the replies and have provided some draft Government
responses, which Margot currently has in her box. She felt the first draft was not accommodating enough of ideas
given. We were hoping the Government response could be released in March. However, officials feel it would be
best to release it along with the funding announcement. Margot has asked if the funding decision will delay
releasing the response. Officials are meeting HMT this week, so should be able to provide a firmer update of the
likelihood of further delays to the funding. This can be fed back to MJ with the original submission (left in the folder
marked ‘useful subs’). Once we have finalised our response, Margot agreed to meet separately with Dave Ward,
Communications Workers Union (CWU - POL Union) and Paula Vennells, CEO of POL to discuss the results.

Strike Action:

The main Union, CWU, have held a number of strike days due to the franchising of certain Post Office branches.
There are a small number of Crown Branches left (approx. 260) that are run by POL. The company have
implemented a programme to franchise the Crowns — especially those that are loss making. This would mean they
will move to be hosted in another shop (independent or something like a Spa or WH Smiths). This only affects a
small number of employees and branches — 260 out of 11,600. It is also bringing these branches in line with the
majority of the network, which is already in this format. The last strike was before Christmas, but if there are further
announcements there is likely to be further Industrial Action. Margot has had a number of meetings/calls with Dave
Ward (CWU) who says he wants to improve communications with POL, using Margot as a mediator. As this is an
operational matter, we should not get involved, as POL are operationally independent. This means any media
surrounding the issue should take the same stance. Margot has a good relationship with Dave Ward — they have
exchanged text messages previously. However, she is still able to take a hard stance with him if she disagrees with
him, or he asking something against the Government's position.

Postal Aff.

This includes all Postal Policy, Royal Mail (RM) and other delivery firms. This is separate to the Post Office —ina
nutshell, RM do the logistical delivery service and the Post Office are the shop front. They are no longer one
company (following the Postal Services Act) but are still heavily connected.

Post Implementation Review of the Postal Services Act:

When the Postal Services Act came into force it included a clause to say there would be a review within a reasonable
amount of time after 5 years. We have carried out the review, and our response is currently out to Write Round.
Once we have agreement to this we can release the report. In the meantime, Ofcom also did a review and released
their report. This was generally positive, so there was no media attention around it.

Tri-lateral relationship — Royal Mail and Ofcom

During our meeting with Sharon White (CEO Ofcom) she agreed to tri-lateral meetings between BEIS, Ofcom and RM

to ensure RM aren’t overburdened as the Universal Service Provider. However, Ofcom have since confirmed they

will not field Sharon for any Tri-laterals. She currently has a number of other pieces of work on that require her

attention (and feels she has put a lot into post recently). Ofcom are content for the Tri-laterals to be a ‘talking shop’,

but if it looks like it will become decision making they will walk away, as they should be operationally independent.

Scam Mail

In October 2016 the Daily Mail ran a news story exposing practices by fraudulent companies sending scam letters
asking for people to send them money (for various reasons). This highlighted postal service operators did not do
enough to prevent this from happening. Margot hosted a roundtable with the main players (RM, Whistl, UK Mail,
Ofcom, National Trading Standards, Medicines and Healthcare products Regulatory Agency, Citizens Advice and
Home Office). They agreed to work together to prevent Scam Mail where possible. It is a difficult area as the
Regulation of Investigatory Powers Act states you are not allowed to open mail with reasonable suspicion. The
postal operators have agreed a Code of Conduct to agree to reduce illegal scam mail. We are also reviewing what
support we can provide from a legal perspective, but this appears limited at this point and would require changes to
legislation. National Trading Standards are compiling a report which is expected in April/May. Margot has agreed to
another stakeholder roundtable after this to reconvene and take stock. This will be a good time as it will be about 6
months after the first meeting.
