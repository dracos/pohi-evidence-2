Proposal for a Working Group Between Post Office
Limited, BEIS and Other Stakeholders.

Purpose

Since becoming independent of Royal Mail, Post Office Limited has maintained a
national network of over 11,500 branches, moved and upgraded over 7,500 branches,
increased opening hours by over 200,000 a week and become the largest network
trading on a Sunday. While Post Office Limited improved its trading profit from a loss
of £120 million in 2012 to an expected profit of £50 million in 2017/18, postmaster
remuneration has been declining and we continue to hear anecdotal evidence of
postmasters’ struggling up and down the country. To ensure that the business
continue building on this success, and to ensure running a Post Office remains an
attractive proposition to postmasters, we recommend setting up a quarterly working
group with Post Office Limited, the National Federation of Sub-Postmasters (NFSP)
and other stakeholders.

Because operational matters — including renumeration and the contractual relationship
between Post Office Limited and postmasters — are sole responsibility of the business,
we suggest the working group focuses on designing a more ambitious, long-term
strategy to raise the profile of the Post Office Limited across Whitehall as a way to
deliver public facing government services. This idea has been tested with both the
NFSP and Post Office Limited and was well received by both. However, in view of the
strong language used by Honourable Mr Justice Fraser to criticise the relationship
between the Post Office and the NFSP, including questioning the independence of the
NFSP, we recommend broadening the list of attendees to include a wider range of
stakeholders with an interest in Post Office matters. These could include the Plunkett
Foundation, the Federation for Small Business, Rural England and the Association for
Convenience Stores. Other Government departments — such as DWP, the Home
Office, HMT, DEFRA, DCMS, MHCLG, HMRC, DfT, GDS and Cabinet Office — could
also be brought into the conversation to discuss specific opportunities.

This approach would allow Government to take a more active role in determining what
Post Office Limited’s purpose ought to be and to explore what new services could Post
Office Limited provide to advance public policy objectives. These could include — for
example — strengthening Post Offices role as ‘community hubs’, tackling loneliness
and isolation, providing pre-paid e-money cards, take deposits and lend to the
vulnerable, authenticate identity, provide pharmacy collection points and other close-
to-home services.

Handling
Should you agree with this proposal, you could reference the creation of this group at
BEIS Oral Questions on 19 March.
