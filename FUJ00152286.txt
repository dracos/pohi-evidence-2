1 MANAGEMENT SUMMARY

Within the Fujitsu Services domain for June 08 there were no Major Incidents, and 6 service-affecting incidents,
2 incidents relating to VIP sites and 2 further service-affecting incidents outside the Fujitsu domain, detailed in
the incident overview below.

Branch and counter availability continue to remain above target at 99.81% and 99.74% respectively. The
Engineering Service continues to show a good performance, with any failing SLT’s only failing by a fraction of a
percentage point.

49 Preventative maintenance visits took place in June. This is proving to be a success with 69% of sites not
logging a hardware call since. There have been no Back Office printer calls logged for any of the sites. The
expectation is that these will reduce the number of incidents and provide an enhanced service to the Post
Office.

There has been continued focus on WH Smith and VIP branches, with 37% of C priority calls logged with
engineering, being attended on the same day.

306 OBC changes were delivered in the month, with one failure due to a PSTN line plant issue. The OBC and
POL teams have met with Avery on site at WH Smiths and have identified an improvement which will prevent
the pin pad being disconnected when the base unit is pulled forward for Scales installation.

The number of engineering visits has seen the lowest average daily rate for 3 years, with a total for the month of
3623. An element of this will be due to the branch closures, however a significant element of this is due to the
sense and respond activity, such as the pin pad refresh button.

The Post Shop SLT for non critical incidents passed at 100% and disappointingly the critical incident SLT did
not, with 2 incidents failing target delivering a performance of 84.6%. Corrective actions are in place to address,
detailed in the Post Shops summary.

The overall number of complaints logged with NBSC has decreased in June to 36, the lowest number for over a
year. With none considered to be justified. Some additional graphs have been added to this Service Review
Book to show trends and associated activities to improve in this area.

No branches failed the Day J targets.

Significant activities from the Service Improvement Plan are included in this review book. A number of the
branch improvements have been in pilot and demonstrated positive results, and these will be rolled out in
agreement with POL over the coming weeks.

5.2 Engineering Service

during this period.

5.3 Branch Network Service / Central Network

5.3.1. Branch Network Service
During June 1505 calls were logged across all Network types, a decrease of 67 calls compared with May.

In total 31 calls failed to hit the 4 hour priority SLA. This gave 97.6% SLT conformance against the 95% target.
We are working closely with all parties to continually improve this figure.

VSAT sites have undergone software changes from System Support Centre (SSC) within RIPOSTE and the
way it reads each satellite site. These changes allow the flow of traffic between the connection and the kit to
occur more constant; the failure rate of transactions has been at its lowest point since introduction.

A conference and training session was held between OBC engineers and the Branch Intervention Managers,
this was to familiarise themselves with new BT methods for site testing. A few of the actions from the conference
include an improved updates to engineers via a new referral form and a checklist of what can be asked of a BT
engineer when they visit problem sites. We will be monitoring the sites that the OBC engineers visit to see if
resolution between third parties is of a shorter timescale.

The combined efforts of Branch Network services, POL and the Communications Management Team have
again seen the GSM bill reduced even further. New scripts and improved reporting are allowing both POL and
Fujitsu Services to efficiently and effectively track GSM usage and monitor the devices both out in the estate
and in engineering holding. PSTN modem tracking will be added to the BNR report in July to further improve
the tracking and return of Branch Network Resilience.

5.3.2 Central Network

This month there were several of intermittent hits on the A&L network banking connectivity that caused a few
thousand transaction timeouts. As a precaution Fujitsu visited the A&L Carlton Park Leicester data centre and
replaced the suspected hardware. However the A&L service is still being monitored very closely around the

Reconciliation issues in June 2008:

The TPSC254 Harvester Exception report of 1° June 2008 contained 4364 exceptions. The problem arose due
to 2 changes to the TPS harvester.

The first change was identified by CP4461. This added new columns to the database and provided a new
harvester to write to the new columns.

The second change is CP4616. This took the harvester from CP4461 and made some further changes.
CP4616 was applied over a weekend but CP4461 was not applied. This meant that the new harvester was
trying to write to database columns that did not yet exist - hence the rejections. CP4616 was withdrawn so
harvesting then took place properly on the evening of 2" June.

The 4364 exceptions were repaired and delivery was finalised/confirmed on 9" June 2008.

The TPSC254 Harvester Exception report of 2" and 3" June showed there were issues where some AP
counter messages were missing or had corrupt start dates. After thorough investigation, these issues were
linked to the DVLA problems as per TfS call 293062 on 2"? June 2008.

There were problems overnight on 17” & 18" June with 18 transactions which caused the TPS processes to
ABEND, in turn holding up the Maestro schedule. The Maestro schedule was re-run on Wednesday morning
and the reports were confirmed as successfully delivered by 10:30am by P&BA in Post Office Ltd. The cause
was investigated and it was found there was a problem with a repair tool. The repair tool was fixed, all
transactions were repaired and the 18 transactions were successfully delivered to POLFS and POLMIS.
