Personally, I deplore the confrontational turn that these negotiations
have taken. I believe that the Horizon system has immense potential
for the benefit of nat only the Post Office and HMG, but also the
citizen and taxpayer. Post Office Counters owns a unique network of
direct contacts with every sector of society including the socially

Looking through the spreadsheets which have been the -pasis for the
discussions to date, I am struck by the pessimism of the projections for
the future volume of POCL's business which they incorporate. Such
pessimistic projections would not be accepted by any commercial
board of directors. If they were changed to those that one would
expect to see in the business case of a commercial enterprise, the
outlook for Post Office Counters would be transformed. The potential
£200 million gap thrown up by the current business model would
become irrelevant.

1 would like to clear up a potential misunderstanding. The ICL
position appears to have been interpreted by the Government as an
absolute requirement for ICL to earn a positive return on the base cash
flows for the project. That is not the case. However, for ICL to be
able to proceed with a base case showing fess than a positive return,
there needs to be a prospect of our being able to recover our position
later on. A solution to this problem will therefore be conditional on
the introduction of commercial management in POCL.

I am troubled by the fact that there is no business case agreed between te
ICL, POCL, the BA and the Treasury based on figures in which all can

have confidence. It would be very helpful if the Government would

appoint an adviser who has the confidence of all the relevant public

sector entities and can work with our financial advisers, SG Hambros,

to produce an agreed business case for POCL and Pathway, taking

account of all the potential new businesses.

So far as the need for greater commercial drive by POCL's
management is concerned, I believe that the way forward is to put in
place an innovative Public Private Partnership which unites the public
service traditions of the Post Office with the commercial flair of the
private sector.
