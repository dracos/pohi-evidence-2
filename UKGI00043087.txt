Post Office Limited (POL): Quarterly update

Summary
1. This is a regular quarterly update on POL, and follows the previous update submitted on 26
September.

Timing
2. Routine. You are meeting with officials on 11 February.

Overview

3. The litigation settlement in December capped a significant year for POL and, while major
challenges remain, there has been tangible progress compared to 12 months ago. A new CEO
was appointed and has made a positive start in setting a new path for the company, linking to the
policy team’s work on a Long Term Vision. POL has signed a new deal with Bank of Ireland and
looks close to finalising a critical agreement with Royal Mail. The agreement of a new Banking
Framework has unlocked a major source of revenue to support the network and is an area where
an active Shareholder has added particular value. While important issues remain with the
convicted claimants, the settlement was a major milestone. In addition, POL continues to make
progress commercially, with trading profits currently ahead of budget.

4. Overall the company is heading into 2020 in better shape than it began 2019. There is a significant
agenda of work in the months ahead. Most critical will be the completion of Nick Read’s Purpose,
Strategy and Growth (PSG) strategy review and the company’s 5 Year Plan as well as the
associated restructuring of the business and management structure. For BEIS and UKGI, our
biggest areas of focus will be ensuring POL’s plans are aligned with the department's vision,
assuring the 5YP, getting funding in place beyond March 2021 and obtaining state aid approval.

5 Year Plan, Spending Review and long-term policy work

5. Long-term policy work: The objectives of this work are to (a) define a clear, relevant and impactful
social purpose and (b) ensure that Government’s network and access criteria help strike the right
balance between supporting POL’s commercial growth and meeting the accessibility requirements
of citizens, particularly the most vulnerable. We therefore have a programme of work underway to
deliver on the post 2021 subsidy; future network sustainability; and role and value of the social
purpose which includes the future government services offer. Separate advice is being provided
to Minister Tolhurst. Following a commission to POL to provide details of their post 2021 funding
request, we are expecting a return in February which will flesh out our understanding of the cost
of the uncommercial parts of the network.

6. 5 Year Plan (SYP). Nick Read has brought in McKinsey to work on the PSG and 5YP. The work
aims to set a new vision for the company, make strategic investment choices (‘big bets’) and clarify
the IT and organisational structures required to deliver the strategy. It will also indicate the
government support POL believe they need to fulfil the commercial strategy and meet their network
commitments.

7. McKinsey’s initial work analyses in much greater detail than before the profitability of different parts
of the branch network and the company overall. It shows that 50% of POL’s network revenues are
driven by just 14% of branches. Around 5,600 of POL’s 11,600 branches are loss-making,
including all DMBs, outreaches and most of the traditional (pre-Network Transformation) branches.
As a result, we expect that POL will seek to accelerate the completion of the franchising
programme.

9. Timetable. Delivery of the 5YP has slipped, with the Board now seeing an outline plan on 28
January, followed by a full discussion in February and sign-off in March. The plan will then require
shareholder approval, which we will seek to obtain by June. We are procuring consultancy support
to assist us in scrutinising and assuring the SYP, as we did for POL’s previous Strategic Plan. This
work will be funded by POL but procured by UKGI/BEIS and we are going through the relevant
process. We will be testing the assumptions underpinning the plan, and ensuring that the targets
for profit, cost efficiencies and cash generation are realistic and sufficiently challenging. POL also
need to convince us that the plan is deliverable and can be bought into by staff and postmasters.

10. Obtaining the relevant State Aid approval and getting the final Funding Agreement and updated
Working Capital Facility (WCF) in place requires careful sequencing, given the potential for the
spending review to be pushed back to the Autumn. The current WCF expires at the end of March
2021. Anew WCF needs to be signed by the end of 2020 so that POL’s accounts for 2019/20 can
be signed off without a going concern qualification. Officials have worked closely on agreeing a
timetable, which is attached separately at Annex D.

. Investment. Nick Read’s letter to you on 4 December stated that POL will not be bidding for any
investment funding. However, the 5YP work is suggesting a need for substantial investment and
we expect that POL will once again request that they are allowed to borrow for investment, either
from Government or private markets. When POL has submitted its SYP we will need to consider
whether there is a justification for allowing to company to borrow from Government for investment
purposes. If so it’s likely that a new borrowing facility will be needed in addition to the existing
WCF.

12. Dividend or subsidy waiver. We are considering options that would allow the Government to
receive a share of future surpluses that POL generates. This could be through an explicit dividend
policy or POL effectively waiving the subsidy it is entitled to. POL have agreed to an aspiration for
the payment of a dividend in future as part of the new Framework Document but believe that they
are some years away from being able to pay a dividend due to the need to finance significant
ongoing investment. POL would need to have sufficient cash flow to be able to pay a dividend,
requiring a shift in focus from EBITDAS to cash generated in the company targets and
remuneration packages. It would also require agreement from Ministers.

Management structure

13.Management team. Nick Read has embarked on changes to his senior leadership team, to
address in capability and to provide a structure better suited to his vision for Post Office. Debbie
Smith, the Head of Retail, has left the business, and the post will disappear as part of the
organisational restructuring. Owen Woodley, the current Head of Financial Services, will move to
a new role with responsibility for all POL’s different products. A new HR Director has been
promoted from within POL while the new Head of Comms is an external hire. Nick asked the
Interim Chief Information Officer to leave at the end of her probation period and a recruitment
process is underway. Nick also plans to recruit externally to give POL much-needed commercial
and strategy expertise. None of these changes require shareholder approval as they are not Board
positions. Nick will present further details to the Board on 28 January. While Nick reports that Al
Cameron has worked well with him after stepping down as Interim CEO, it remains unclear whether
Al will continue as CFOO in the long term. Al will become eligible for his six-month retention
payment in March.

Legal

14.‘Sparrow’ litigation. On 11 December POL agreed a settlement of I to settle the GLO
proceedings. The settlement also includes other elements aimed at strengthening POL’s
relationship with its postmasters. This followed the Court of Appeal’s decision to reject on all
grounds POL’s permission to appeal the ‘Common Issues’ Judgment (22 Nov) and was followed
shortly afterwards by the ‘Horizon Issues’ Judgment (16 Dec). The settlement announcement
received balanced coverage, but there has been some noise from claimants unhappy with the
payment they received once the funders had been paid and legal fees deducted. On 4 January
Alan Bates (lead claimant in the GLO) wrote to Kelly Tolhurst expressing discontent at the final fee
received. This was accompanied by an invoice of £47m. The story was also picked up by Computer
Weekly who have asked the Department for comment. Advice was submitted on 14 January noting
these developments and proposing a firm but sympathetic “no” to the claimants’ request for
payment following UKGI and BEIS legal advice.

15.As you know, POL is still potentially exposed to further claims from i) the cohort of convicted
claimants, and ii) other postmasters who were not part of the GLO. Now that the litigation has been
settled, the “Further Issues” trial that was scheduled for March will no longer go ahead.

19. Network performance and sustainability. POL continues to meet its network commitments. At the
end of November 2019, there were 11,613 post office branches, above the minimum of 11,500
and POL met the geographical access criteria (Annex C). In addition to the programme of
franchising its directly managed branches, POL plans to open 220 new post offices in 2019/20
(165 have opened YTD). However, in recent years POL has only been able to meet its network
commitment through a sustained increase in the use of outreach branches, which open for as little
as a few hours a week. There are important policy questions about the minimum branch standards
and the degree of reliance on outreaches that will be considered as part of the long-term policy
work and SR bid.

20. Some of POL’s network partners operate multiple branches, with McColl’s hosting over 600. The
potential for one of these partners to hit financial difficulty is a risk to POL’s ability to sustain the
network and UKGI have encouraged POL to analyse the financial stability of its partners and build
mitigation plans. While we are satisfied with their plans, if a partner of this size were to get into
financial difficulties it could lead to disruption.

Other business issues

21. Agent Pay. POL’s Agent Pay review delivered its final report to the Board in October. It proposed
increases worth £33m per year, on top of the £17m announced in August, but we were
disappointed with the evidence underpinning the recommendations. This highlighted both POL’s
recurring weakness in the use of data and the need to get a better grip on postmaster economics
— a point we have made repeatedly to management and the Board. Our concern is that the
increase would not have been directed to the parts of the network that needs it most. The Board
agreed to a lower uplift of £20m, payable from FY 20/21, with the components of the increase to
be agreed. Nevertheless, the news was well-received by the NFSP. Further work to improve the
relationship with postmasters and retail partners will form part of the 5YP.

22. Royal Mail. POL is close to finalising a new non-exclusive deal with Royal Mail to replace the

25. IT — Belfast Exit and PCI. POL is seeking to migrate two key elements of its IT — the Horizon
application and the Oracle database - to the cloud from a physical datacentre in Belfast on which
the lease runs out in December 2021. This is a complex process but once completed it will reduce
costs and improve service. Having paused the project in the Autumn, POL is now pressing ahead
after it transpired that Oracle needs to be upgraded before technical support is withdrawn in
December 2020. Once Oracle is upgraded and migrated to the cloud, there will be a significant
period of testing before Horizon can be migrated and POL can exit the datacentre. A further
complication is the pressing need to achieve compliance with PCI regulations, since PCI is
currently a component within the Horizon system. The timetable for completing the migration is
tight, but POL believes it is achievable. The greater risk is probably achieving PCI compliance
before the end of 2021 deadline. Both projects will be top of the in-tray for POL’s new ClO.

26. BF2 and Barclays. The new Banking Framework began on 1 January and includes Barclays, who
reversed their decision to opt out of the cash withdrawal service following significant pressure from
the media, Ministers and other MPs. It has become increasingly apparent that the provision of
cash banking is central to both POL’s social purpose and its commercial viability. As such, it is vital
that government sets a clear vision for access to cash and the obligations on banks to support this.
This will be a focus for our engagement with HMT over the coming months.

27. Cash logistics. Post Office’s supply chain business manages the cash that it provides to branches,
handling an average of £1.5bn every day. Having reviewed the business in Summer 2019, POL
see opportunities for efficiencies in the short to medium term, while they wait for HMG’s ambitions
for a ‘cash utility’ to come to fruition. POL has been discussing outsourcing options with several
partners and would like to move towards the development of business cases. Such a move would
be unpopular with unions and pose execution challenges, but POL believe the risks are
manageable and the commercial upside significant. However there is considerable political
interest in the cash agenda and HMT are nervous that any movement by POL could undermine a

Governance

29.Culture_and stakeholder views. As part of Nick Read’s ‘Purpose, Strategy and Growth’ work,
McKinsey conducted their Organisational Health survey, which has now been discussed
extensively with POL’s employees. The results were very poor. POL had third or fourth quartile
scores in nearly every area and staff currently lack a clear sense of engagement and
accountability. McKinsey have also conducted surveys and focus groups with postmasters and
customers. All stakeholders believe that trust is a key asset of the Post Office. Postmasters also
ranked serving the local community very highly, but this was not reflected in customer feedback.
Customers valued Post Office’s reputation and convenient location, but scores were lower for the
customer experience. Customer awareness of non-mail products was weak and all scores were
considerably lower for younger age groups.

30. Future of STIP and LTIP. POL’s Remco has approved financial targets for the 19/20 STIP (£59.2m/
On Target: £74m / Stretch: £88.8m) and the 19/22 LTIP (£93.5m/110m/126.5m). The bonus
payments themselves are unchanged from those agreed with the CST at the time of the CEO’s
appointment. We are seeking approval in time to meet end of year bonuses. Remco have also
proposed a new set of metrics for future years and changes to strengthen their discretion to
clawback bonuses. There is potential for Unite to raise issues as the changes will apply to ail
employees covered by the STIP, not just the CEO and CFO. POL do not envisage an issue but
are monitoring.

Government policy

36. Verify / Digital ID. As you are aware, Cabinet Office have asked POL to pay a higher fee to
Digidentity to stop them making losses and potentially leaving the market. Currently GDS pays
POL £2 per user sign up (due to drop to £1 soon), of which £1.70 is passed onto Digidentity.
However, it costs Digidentity £4.50 to issue a digital identity to a POL customer. Requiring POL to
pay £4.50 per user would turn a 30p profit into a £2.50 loss - a loss of £4.2m per year based on
POL’s projections of securing 1.5m users a year. Post Office do not want to bear this loss and are
unconvinced by Cabinet Office’s promises about the potential of a future digital identity
‘ecosystem’. A further meeting is being set up between POL and John Manzoni in February. In the
meantime, Digidentity is threatening to stop accepting new registrations on POL’s behalf in the
next few weeks, which, according to POL, is a breach of contract. We believe the key to resolving
this issue is for HMG to be clear with POL about its future ambitions and how participants in Verify
could benefit as this would enable POL to make a commercial decision to continue to invest in the
business. Our views have been communicated to Cabinet Office.

37.Post Office Card Account (POca). POL believe that DWP’s plans for POca will have significant
negative impacts on both customers and postmasters and they have informed us that they are not
minded to bid for the replacement service. They also expect increase in public commentary about
the implications of DWP moving to a voucher scheme in the next few months. It is highly likely that
postmasters and stakeholders will react negatively to services being withdrawn from the Post
Office network, with specific areas of the country (Northern Ireland in particular) being severely
affected. However, POL’s plan for doing so will not be finalised until the POca steering group meets
next Thursday. BEIS officials have been in discussion with DWP counterparts to make sure they
are aware of these risks, ahead of their planned procurement for POca’s replacement, which is
now due to go live in March 2021.

Revenue

Revenue is down on budget. Higher revenue in Retail and Identity, predominantly driven by mails and
banking, has been offset by weakness in Financial Services, Telecoms and Insurance. This is mainly
due to lower than expected Bank of Ireland commissions, weak demand for foreign exchange and
overseas travel. Performance in Post Office Insurance has also been disappointing.

Costs

Overall costs were favourable to budget, particularly non-staff costs. Half of this was due to IFRS15
accounting adjustments that require costs to be recognised over the lifetime of the contract and the
bulk of the remainder is due to the reallocation of GLO costs to other cost lines. Cost of Sales and
Agent Pay are below budget as a function of weaker revenues.
