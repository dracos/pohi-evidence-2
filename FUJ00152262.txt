2 JULY REVIEW OF SERVICE

2.1 MANAGEMENT SUMMARY

The service remained stable in July, and there were no major incidents. Overall Branch and Counter availability are being sustained at 99.9% and
99.6% respectively.

Call volumes to the helpdesk are maintaining the downward trend shown last month, and continuing the reduction in all major areas over the May
and June figures. However, hardware call volumes remain high with counter printers still the most significant element of this, but stable. Engineer
onsite visits also remain high but stay at a steady rate.

The engineering SLT performance reflects the high volume and was exacerbated by some spares & resource management issues. These are being
addressed and the backlog reduced. However, this has led to a rise in complaints especially relating to engineer ETA and repeat visits. The service
delivery and operations teams are working together closely to ensure expectations are managed and met.

Extreme weather conditions caused a call influx on a number of days in July, but these were managed as part of business as usual, and no major
incidents resulted from this.

Performance on calls logged for Online Service provision has started to improve again, following the refocus on managing the resolution of
intermittent calls. The proportion of calls closed within 24 and 48 hours has shown an upturn.

There was a low volume of OBC work, at 140 this month. Although the number of failure is still low, the failure rate was 8.7% against the new
OLA of 4% minus closures. A number of corrective actions have been discussed and are in place to ensure that this stays under control.

2.1.2 Availability and Problem Management

Summary: Within the Fujitsu Services domain for July 2006 there were no Major Incidents. There was a further incident effecting service outside
the Fujitsu domain.

Major Incidents: None

Other incidents in Fujitsu Services domain:

Monday 24th July. Early morning showed a higher than normal outage of ADSL branches due to heavy storms across the country at the weekend.
At 08:15 hrs there were 546 branches unable to communicate, at 08:45 206 branches, 09:15 161 branches. At 10:15 hrs 97 branches were down and.
were managed according to normal business processes.

VIP branches affected this month:

Monday 24th July. VIP branch 015010 Trafalgar Square, gateway was communicating at start of day, but no counters working. Branch suffered a
lightning strike which affected the hubs and cables. Engineers replaced first hub and had 6 counters working by 13:00 and 11 counters by the end of
the day. Required further engineering visits the next day Tuesday to replace further hubs and cable, all 23 counters returned to service by 16:58.

Incidents that were outside the Fujitsu Domain:

Sunday 9th July. There was a Major Incident at the DVLA from 11:30 on Sunday 9th July to 11:00 Thursday 13th July. The On-Line EVL &
BART Systems were unavailable during this time. This was due to database issues, which had to be restored with the help of Oracle & EMC. MVL
at branches were in manual fallback mode.

Monday 17th July. Link failures experienced between 17:09 to 18:15. Timeouts of transactions experienced at a rate of 24%. This was due to a Co-
op bank issue at these times.

2.1.3 OBC Branch Change Service Summary

In July 2006 there were a total of 140 changes undertaken, this is a decrease of over 100 changes compared to July 2005. 7 of these were cancelled
changes. With the exclusion of ‘Closures’ 92 changes were undertaken. Fujitsu were responsible for 8 change requests not being completed on the
target date. For Fujitsu this represents a failure rate of 8.70% against the new 4% OLA minus Closures. It also represents a failure rate of 6.02%
against the original target of 5%. There were no line plant failures; this also represents a failure rate of 6.02% against the old 4% target excluding
Line Plant. There is a difference of -6.16% compared to June’s Target without Closures. Compared to the June calculations a difference of -4.03%
against the 5% target rate and a difference of -4.03% against the 4% target rate, excluding line plant.

2.1.8 Horizon Service Desk and Complaints

In July, Fujitsu Services received 44 complaints from the NBSC, 29 of which were passed through for information only. Of the remaining 13, 10
were found to be justified following investigation by Fujitsu Services, and all of which were complaints regarding the time taken for engineers to
attend site and the number of visits necessary to resolve the faults.

Fujitsu Services is continuing the work started in previous months to improve the information provided to Post Masters regarding the expected
arrival time of the engineer, to ensure that we are setting and managing the customer expectation. HSD agents are checking for long standing
engineering calls and escalating to the engineering managers to ensure that focus is placed on these branches.

Counter Printer calls are still running higher than forecast, which is continuing to have an impact on the scheduling of engineering visits, and the
availability of spares. In addition to the actions detailed above, we are working closely with the third party printer supplier to bring extra stock into
the spares loop and to train additional staff to meet the demand for replacement printers. A fix has also been developed for a software fault, which
impacts the counter printers and has gone through successful testing and pilot and was introduced to the branch infrastructure on 10" August. The
“instruction labels” for printing Postal Orders have been distributed and we are seeing an improvement in the number of reported faults relating to
POs.

2.1.9 Business Incident Management

There were 31 reconciliation incidents during July. This is 9 less than that reported in June 2006.

All 31 reconciliation incidents were within the 5 day response SLT.
There were no 8 hour response SLT incidents.

2.1.10 Engineer Services — Site Visits By Engineer

Although remaining high overall July has seen again another slight decrease in the amount of visits by engineers. Visits for the majority of components have
remained consistent with June but we have seen slight decreases in the number of visits for Counter Printers as Post Masters and HSD alike grow more
confident with the actions needed to resolve certain issues.
Adhesive labels have been distributed to engineering and these are now being applied to printer casings across the estate aiding in fault resolutions.

Fujitsu eagerly awaits authorisation from Post Office Limited to begin the Epson printer roll out and understands a decision can be expected before the end of
August. The size and nature of this roll out will also be determined at this point.
Fujitsu LST testing of the new base-lined Pin Pad proved successful as has live testing at Calthorpe House. Hypercom have been given the green light to
proceed with the provision of these new base-lined units. As a result configuration times have been reduced to between 6 and 10 minutes.

*All the graphs and tables within this section show all calls logged and do not include any call exclusions.

There were no Fujitsu major incidents in July 2006 and 272 of the total 1563 network calls were related to an outage at the DVLA. Call volumes
were also impacted by poor weather conditions causing Post Office branch power outages and BT local exchanges faults.

The alert project to provide additional core network capacity inside the Fujitsu datacentres is now complete and the Fujitsu networks team are now
working on upgrading and standardising the firewall configurations to provide better resilience.

The new ADSL IPStream network is providing a very stable service with a total of 627 branches live at the end of July 2006. Incidents relating to
this new service are recorded by the HSD using the new NDO3 coding shown in the powerhelp detailed call breakdown.
