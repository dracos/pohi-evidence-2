Note of Interview with Ken Alan Crawley, Post Officer, Chesterfield.

Kenneth has worked as a Post Officer in the Pensions and Accounts Team for three years.
He has worked overall for the Post Officer for thirty five years. Kenneth explained as follows

1. He works on the Agent Debt team and has been on the team for approaching three years.
The job includes dealing with cases remaining from the Pensions and Allowances work
carried out for the (now) Department For Work and Pensions. The Agent Debt Team issue
invoices to Postmasters for debt and finish off P & A cases. They check work for DWP.

They look after P & A error notices and when other people issue other error notices, in other
departments, they monitor and control them to progress payments.

The actual Pension and Allowance counterfoils are submitted by the Post Office branches on
a weekly basis through the Paid Order Unit at Lisahally, Northern Ireland. The Paid Order
Unit is responsible for conducting checks of the Paid counterfoils received. When somebody
goes to collect their pension payment from the Post Office, the branch will stamp their book
and also stamp the counterfoil that is produced. They will pay the money out in cash to the
person. The branch name and date is shown on an indicator mark, indicating which counter
clerk saw the customer, and this is contained on the stamp. (There is no indicator mark for
the Moon Drive Branch). The Sub-Postmaster completes a daily summary of these
counterfoils which is Form P2311MA and these together with the foils are sent to Ireland. In
addition a further copy of Form P2311MA is sent to what was then “transaction processing”.

What may not be known amongst the other branches is that the Paid Order Unit does not
conduct every foil received The Form P2311MA is generated by Horizon system but the
Postmaster must check that against the counterfoils. Generally, the Paid Order Unit receives
thousands of foils each day and could not possibly check them all, therefore they do
samples. They manually check 4-500 a week on a rota basis. There were at its peak,
18,000 Sub-Post Office branches and therefore each branch usually gets checked once every
two years. Kenneth was able to connect the old system for me and confirmed that during
the material period, the Moon Drive Branch was checked on rota in Week 44.

If the checks reveal any discrepancies, the Paid Order Unit would notify the Transaction
Processing Team. They would do this by entering details into the Pacsys Computer System.
Once a week Transaction Processing downloading that information from Pacsys. Once
Transaction Processing received the paperwork relating to the discrepancy, the information
from Pacsys will be automatically inputted into the Counter Business Database. (This is not
Horizon). Any supporting documentation, such as P2311MA summary sheet would be

The error notice is formal notification to the Branch advising of a mistake made and giving
instructions on how to rectify the situation on their account. It is sent to the Branch in
question and the Post Master is required to bring the error notice to account before the end
of the next cash account period. Once it has been brought to account, the error notice
should be signed by the Post Master and returned to Transaction Processing together with
cash account. Transaction Processing no longer exists as such, as it is now called Product
and Branch Accounting. However, there are certain occasions when discrepancies are found
by the Paid Order Unit and not dealt with this way. For example if they are for over £80.00,
and the discrepancy is believed not to have been caused by general mistakes, relevant
documentation is forwarded to the Post Office Ltd Investigation Section. An error notice
would not then be issued unti! the completion of any investigation and the Paid Order Unit
will manually check each of the counterfoils and summary sheets received for the following
four weeks.

The Pacsys information will contain the office code, the week number, the week ending,
details of the error including the value and the group it falls into (for example sickness
benefit, income support, family credit etc). Pacsys stands for Pension and Allowances
Checking System.
