1 MANAGEMENT SUMMARY

Within the Fujitsu Services domain for Dec 07 there were no Major Incidents and 1 service-affecting incident, no
incidents relating to VIP sites and 4 further service-affecting incidents outside the Fujitsu domain, detailed in the
incident overview below.

Branch and counter availability continue to remain above target at 99.80% and 99.43% respectively. Despite
this, December proved to be a very difficult month with some disappointing results in some areas.

The pilot of the revised Engineering service targets has continued and disappointingly the trend on SLT
performance is down. This is a very disappointing result, initially caused by the helpdesk toolset failure and
subsequently challenges with resource on both helpdesks and also with Engineering. The issues have been
documented and initial actions put in place to stabilize the service, with a subsequent full root cause analysis to
be completed in January. The focus remains on improving Response times, which will enable the overall fix
times to meet target.

49 OBC changes were delivered in the month, with no failures.

The Critical hours SLT for Post Shops failed in December with 1 incident missing target and the non-critical SLT
also failed due to a helpdesk issue.

2 of the helpdesk Service Level Targets failed, calls answered within 30 seconds and calls not answered. This is
very disappointing and the first time in 12 months that we have seen this. A shortage of headcount on the desk
has caused this, and this has been addressed, with new agents being progressed through the training
programme. They are expected to be ready in the next 2 weeks.

The overall number of complaints logged with NBSC has increased in December and this can be attributed to
the issues encountered by our helpdesks and engineering. Fujitsu Services complaints owner and Post Office
Limited complaints owner have met to re-instigate the full review process agreed in May 2007 and as such we
will expect the dashboard to show an increased number of complaints going forward.

No branches failed the Day J targets, despite the challenges encountered by engineering and the helpdesks,
and the actions to improve escalation and call management have been successful.

Significant activities from the Service Improvement Plan are included in this review book. A number of the
branch improvements have been in pilot and demonstrated positive results, and these will be rolled out in
agreement with POL over the coming weeks.

Two key Fujitsu Services personnel left the Account during December to progress into new opportunities and
the Operations Director met both her new team and also the Post Office Limited team.

Both Fujitsu and Services have gone through a personnel restructure and new relationships are being forged to
maintain our excellent working relationship.

5.2 Engineering Service

Call volumes throughout December were at expected levels with the exception of base units which is being
investigated. There was a total of 548 A priority calls logged during this period.

Due to a number of issues we experienced a fault with Triole for Service resulting in communications to
engineering, across the OTI link, not working. We reverted to manual processes, which hampered our ability
to be able to manage the calls through the normal call flow process. It was evident that the process had not
been tested for some time, and many of our newer personnel were not familiar with it. This created a back
log of incidents when we came into the peak period, significantly impacting our ability to deliver the service to
SLT. To compound the issue, engineering had some resource challenges, particularly in the Midlands and
also lost two key operational managers to sickness over the Christmas period. There will be a root cause
analysis meeting on 24" January to investigate further and to provide actions to prevent reoccurrence in the
future.

During this busy period, we worked with our engineering supplier to add extra focus to the higher than
normal level of calls highlighted by POL.

To address the high WIP, our engineering supplier called in additional engineers and utilised our OBC and
FMS engineers. In addition to this, regular conference calls were held between the engineering supplier and
the account team to manage the situation. A proactive review is taking place so we can look at ways to work
smarter should the above issues arise again.

5.3 Branch Network Services / Central Network
5.3.1 Branch Network Services

During December there were 749 calls logged across all Network Types into the HSD.

The ‘Priority A —- 6 hours’ SLT saw 18 failures from a total of 749 closed calls measured, resulting in 97.6% SLT
achievement. This was a decrease of 1.6% from the previous month. The decrease has been pinned down to an
issue with the OTI; the issue caused a lot of calls to go un-coded, without a ‘WH’ reference number. This meant
that the calls were ‘stuck’ between two stacks and thus duplicate calls had to be sent across, many of which
were acted upon as they were manually sent to the correct stacks. Some calls between stacks (due to the OTI
issue) went unmonitored and resulted in a delay in order to gain corrective action and an engineer visit. A
workaround has now been created to ensure that the issue, if it does reoccur, can be instantly rectified.

Branch Network Services are working closely with the HelpDesk and with Engineering to ensure that all calls are
dealt with as swiftly as possible. This initiative includes email reporting and conference calls to highlight any
incidents that require any support from either of the networks.

The referral process, in which sites are escalated to POL, has been changed in accordance with an agreement
between POL and Fujitsu. In cases where it has been proven by a Fujitsu engineer that the fault lies with BT
wiring/extensions/NTES/NTE2000, the Comms Management Team (CMT) within Fujitsu will complete the
standard BT form with the relevant information. This is then sent to POL for progression to CMT, eliminating the
duplication of effort from CMT, BNS and POL, which reduces the time to fix and results in a better overall
service.

We are also working closely with Engineering and the CMT to consolidate relevant engineering documents with
helpdesk material and create a Branch Network Service Guide. This will enable engineers and HelpDesk agents
to improve the productivity of site visits, in turn reducing the number of return visits.

5.3.2 Central Network

The Access Layers for ADSL (IP Stream), ISDN (Metered) and VSAT branches remained extremely stable
during December. There was a LAN incident in which a subtle memory leak on the Leic-2 router on 27/12/07
caused 108 transaction reversals on the A&L Bootle agent. Cisco has advised that this is an occasional
characteristic of their software products and not to swap hardware or upgrade the router operating system
unless the issue occurs again on this device. The router has been monitored daily and no problem symptoms
have reoccurred.

The new WAN links to FS STE04 (HSD/SMC) and CSC Maidstone (EDG2 D/R) were installed during
December. Prism are currently resolving their ‘Zinc Whiskers’ issues before the EDG2 D/R server install can be
scheduled. Once complete the remainder of FTMS based AP Clients (ISDN and Leased Line) can be migrated
to EDG in preparation for HNG.

7 Sense & Respond

The data captured during December has highlighted the two highest demand types as Hardware and Software.
Trend analysis has shown that the total call volume for Hardware during December decreased by 1952 calls
(total volume of 8092) and total call volume for Software calls decreased by 421 calls (total volume of 2000).

The total volume of Pin Pad calls for December was 994, with the highest call type being ‘Smart Card reader
faults’ (430 calls).

Hypercom are to provide a sample of their Pin Pad repair analysis (including serial number) to HSD so these
units can be cross referenced with the original problem description, in an attempt to improve frontline
diagnostics/FMS recommendations.

The highest call volume received for Hardware calls is for Counter Printer faults (3104 calls received in
December — a decrease of 1100 calls from November).

HSD are to collate call data on consumable calls and discuss with POL the feasibility of reissuing the printer
LED descriptions to the estate via Operational Focus.

During December, 3147 chase calls were received (+368 calls from November), with the highest chase call type
being Customer Calls (1128 calls). 1011 engineering update chase calls were received during December.

An additional objective regarding engineering calls is to reduce the amount of calls to the HSD from Touch,
Fujitsu and ROMEC engineers.

The Call volume for the HSD has decreased slightly from November to December by 229 calls (1468 received),
however, CMT have experienced an increase of 419 calls (902 calls received).

1336 monitor fault calls were logged during December (-245 calls from November). Of these, 1020 faults (76%)
were resolved by engineering and 316 faults were (24%) resolved by the HSD.

HSD will be reviewing current monitor KELs that exist on the desk and will be liaising with engineering to identify
additional checks that can be conducted.

A project monitoring Inappropriate calls between the HSD and NBSC desks achieved its objective of reducing
the number of calls between the desks from 10% to 5%. However, during November, HSD experienced an
increase in the total number of inappropriate calls received (1809 calls) which represented 11.2% of all calls.
After investigation, the rise in Inappropriate calls appeared to have been caused by a MoneyGram issue and by
the NBSC changing its call/support options.

After tracking Inappropriate calls data throughout December, the total volume of calls received at the HSD was
924, a decrease of 885 calls from November. This represents 7.3% of all calls.
