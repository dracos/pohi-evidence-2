Summary of West End Post Office Scenario

This is a case about a postmistress (PM) who had lost the original error notice demanding a
copy of the same at a time when POL no longer had a copy of the same.

According to Paul Dann there is no suggestion on the e-mails he has located that HORIZON was
said to be to blame in any respect.
11.06.03 - PM made call to NBSC asking: "HOW ARE POL CHEQUES REMMED OFF THE SYSTEM".

Tier 1 advisor told the PM to reverse the rem out as a voucher to CRU however the call was passed to a
tier 2 advisor that gave the PM the correct advice.

As the incorrect advice was rectified by the tier 2 advisor on the same call the advice would not be classed
as incorrect.

Dorothy Kiernan looked at all calls in June and her colleagues have looked at his cash account and cannot
find any problems with a POL cheque.

The problem seems to be linked to reversals: One call I pulled off dated 11/06/03 the advisor asked the
PM to print a transaction log to check if any transactions had been duplicated the PM told the advisor that
the same transaction was showing 8 times he was advised to reverse out the ones not relevant.

Sarah Pennington, Alan Staves and Andrew Price all looked through the information sent by the Sub-
postmaster at West End. They all agreed that the discrepancy being carried from week 11/03 was not
caused by an error in remittances, of a POCL cheque or a voucher to CRU.

If a POCL cheque is remmed into the system and then a voucher to CRU is remmed out, this would set the
system cash figure back to its original level and would not cause a mis-balance. If then the SPMR pays the
pensions out and settles to cash, but gives the POCL cheque to the nursing home staff, the system will
have a gain of the amount of the cheque.

They also noticed a large discrepancy in postage, which could be an underlying factor in the discrepancy
the office still has.

Andrew Price explaining, using figures from memory as an example-:

Week 11 - postage declaration £5,000.

Week 12 - Remittance in of postage £1700, however the postage dec was around £7,800.

Week 13 - postage dec £5,000.

Although over the three weeks this created and corrected a discrepancy it is a concern, unless there's a
genuine reason for this, around the figures the PM is using to balance. He highlighted the above

declarations and remittance on the cash accounts that have been returned.

The PM claimed that his original discrepancy was due to a POCL cheque, however he could not see any
evidence from the cash accounts to support the PM's claim.
