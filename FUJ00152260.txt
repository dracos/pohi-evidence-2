2 MAY REVIEW OF SERVICE

2.1 MANAGEMENT SUMMARY

Fujitsu service levels for May 2006 were impacted in a number of areas following a major network incident on 2 May. Specifically, Service Levels
relating to Calls Answered at the helpdesk and Data & File Delivery (Day B & Day J) were affected.

The root cause of the incident was identified as an error made during a minor network configuration change, resulting in network congestion and
ultimately service loss. Remedial actions have been identified and are being implemented: - short term control on changes and system monitoring are
already in place, and network upgrades to address capacity issues are in hand.

Hardware incidents continue to run at high volumes. Specific focus on printer issues has begun to address problems arising from the new Postal Orders,
and the trends have shown a steady recovery, including an increasing proportion of issues being fixed remotely. However, other root causes are still
being addressed, with input from helpdesk, engineering and user communities. Pin Pad volumes remain high, with most calls being fixed remotely, and a
fix for this is being tested.

The OBC volume remained steady with a small increase in May. There was a failure rate of 3.38% against the new OLA of 4% minus closures, an
improvement of 1.8% over the April performance.

The engineer service performance was affected by the exceptionally high call volume, with 5851 calls requiring onsite visits, and this resulted in some
spares management issues. These were addressed during the latter half of May, and performance has begun to improve towards the end of the month.

The number of faults logged for On line service provision was also affected by the 2" May major incident and issues in the A&L and Link domains
during the month, but the overall times to resolve were improved.

Branch and Counter Availability remained high, and following the introduction of Branch Network Resilience, continues to improve.

2.1.2 Availability and Problem Management

Summary: Within the Fujitsu Services domain for May 2006 there was 1 Major Incident. There were 3 incidents effecting VIP sites. There were 4 further service
effecting incidents which were outside of the Fujitsu domain.

Major Incidents:

Tuesday 2" May. Major Incident, network congestion caused by CAT I (Catalyst) switch incident. Incident started at 11:41 affecting timeouts with On-Line services.
Congestion continued throughout the day with a total loss of on-line services from 14:35 to 15:35 when the CAT I switch was shut down in a controlled manner and
the NPS service was lost. From 15:35 a 50% average of transactions were successfully completed. Incident lasted to 02:30 the following morning whilst the
correspondence servers were re-synchronized. This affected the overnight harvesting and file deliveries. Files were produced but did not contain any transactions.
Service was restored at 02:30 on the 3". Harvesting and data transactions have been recovered and control fed to TPS over the next 2 nights to catch up. MIR E-
0605020722 refers.

VIP sites affected this month.

Saturday 20" May. VIP site Eccles 171941, loss of on-line services. Joint visit resolved on the 23". Call E-0605200154 refers.
Monday 22" May. VIP site Manchester 003422 closed due to flooding. Re-opened 09:00 on the 24" May. Call E-0605220345 refers.
Wednesday 24" May. VIP site Hartlepool 011327. Key Refresh issue at start of day 09:00, resolved at 09:45. Call E-0605240101 refers.

Incidents Outside the Fujitsu Domain:

Wednesday 10" May. The LINK system experienced timeouts. This was due to a MI within the CO-OP & Smile Bank. 2672. Time outs from 12:49 to 13:59. Call E-
0605100673 refers.

Thursday 25" May. The LINK system experienced timeouts. This was due to a total loss of service to the Co-op/Smile bank. The service loss was from 09:30 to
12:05, and 9348 transactions were affected. Call E-0605250278 refers.

Thursday 25" May. The A&L system experienced transaction timeouts at approximately 30% rate. Started at 11:44 to 12:20 & 12:27 till 12:40 and then continued all
through the afternoon. Established that A&L technician had made a change. Total timeouts for the day where 18,799. A&L regressed the change and both A&L and
POA technicians applied “Sniffer” traces to the Network at the time of failures. POA removed its “Sniffer” trace overnight. A&L timeouts again started at 09:10 to
09:50 on Friday the 26", this was attributed to the A&L technicians disconnecting their “Sniffer” traces at this time.

Wednesday 31“ May. DVLA timeouts experienced at a rate of 8% to 11%, from 11:38 to 11:45 and 12:00 to 12:05. POA checked Network and no faults were
identified on network, timeouts were to both Bootle & Wigan. Call placed to the DVLA through the PACT service help desk and the response from the DVLA, was
that a possible overload of the VOSA system occurred at this time. Call E-0605310414 refers.

2.1.3 OBC Branch Change Service Summary

May 2006 realised a total of 185 changes undertaken, 38 of these were cancelled changes. With the exclusion of ‘Closures’ 148 changes were
undertaken. Fujitsu were responsible for 5 change requests not being completed on the target date. For Fujitsu this represents a failure rate of 3.38%
against the new 4% OLA minus Closures. It also represents a failure rate of 3.40% against the original target of 5%. There were no line plant failures;
this also represents a failure rate of 3.40% against the old 4% target excluding Line Plant. There is an improvement of 1.84% compared to April’s Target
without Closures. Compared to the April calculations an improvement of 1.15% against the 5% target rate and 0.50% against the 4% target rate,
excluding line plant.

Post Office Ltd were responsible for 3 change requests not being completed on the target date, which represents 2.04% against the 4% target, which
shows a minimal difference against the April calculations of -0.09%.

Fujitsu’s direct failures comprised of 2 Communications, 1 Software, 1 Hardware & 1 Process.
Post Office Limited direct failures comprised of I Communications I Compliance and I Process.

There was 1 Normal Business Incident.

Due to the operational issue on Tuesday the 2"! of May the Router and LAN operational tests were postponed and are now planned to be performed in late June. During May

2.1.8 Horizon Service Desk and Complaints

In May the top 3 Service areas receiving complaints were online services, Counter Printers and Engineering Support - timeliness of visit & general
conduct. In addition to the specific improvement actions detailed in the table below, all three Service areas have Service Improvement Plans to address
the issues raised which are discussed at the regular Service Review meetings to ensure progress is made against each item.

Against each Service Area the key areas of focus are:

Online Services

The availability of on-line services is subject to considerable focus within Fujitsu Service and Post Office Service Improvement team, with a joint
Continuous Service Improvement Plan in place. Estate wide Branch availability is at its highest level with average monthly availability in excess of
99.65%. We are working to fine tune the Incident Management process to ensure that the resolution of on-line services issues is as effective and efficient
as possible.

The introduction of the Branch Network Resilience allows us to distribute GSM modems to branches without online services, to restore Service as
quickly as possible. The deployment of the modem is subject to GSM Service availability in the geographical region of the affect branch.

In cases where online services unavailability is extended due to the nature of the investigation, or investigation is outside our direct control, we are
working with the Post Office Service Continuity team to ensure that the Post Master is kept informed of progress of the call by providing regular updates.

Counter Printers

In May we saw a considerable increase in calls being logged for counter printers. Daily volumes increased by approximately 100 calls per day. This
resulted in an increase in demand for engineer visits of around 50 per day above normal, and a proportional increase in demand for replacement printers,
of around 40 units per day. The increase in call volume appeared to be related to user difficulty in feeding through the order through the slip and printing
the Postal Order to the required standard.

In order to mitigate against this increase we introduced additional printers into the engineers' spares loop, worked with the printer repairer to increase
output (including full weekend working) and by conducted service visits on Saturday mornings wherever possible. In spite of this, demand for
replacements exceeded supply and this led to a backlog of printer visits, which unfortunately impacted the branches. These initial actions saw a
reduction in call volumes however we continue to monitor this on a daily basis and ongoing Service improvements include, the HSD checking and
specifying the correct slip insertion technique, sending out instructions to engineers to enable them to test the slip feed again before replacing, and to
advise users where necessary on the technique, Post Office Limited sending out written communications to all branches (initially via MBS and then more
detailed paper based instructions), Branch Issues Managers carrying out branch visits for branches known to have repeated problems and retrieving
printers for further analysis.

Engineer Support

Unfortunately the increased call / visit volumes relating to the counter printers had a knock-on impact with other engineering visit. As the Counter
Printer volume trend moves towards lower levels this should also see an improvement in the timeliness of engineering visits. In addition the engineering
force is asked to call ahead to confirm visit times with the branch, which will improve the management of the expectation of the Post Master.

We continue to reiterate to the engineers the importance of their conduct when attending a branch. Regular communication cascades and training
sessions are scheduled with the engineers to ensure that they do not lose sight of this and reduce the number of complaints relating to conduct.

2.1.10 Engineer Services — Site Visits By Engineer

The number of visits by engineers continues to be high. The increase in the number of counter printers was due in part to the introduction of the Postal
Orders, this issue is being managed through various measures and trends show that the number of calls related to Postal Orders coming in to HSD and
requiring an engineer visit is decreasing.

The number of visits related to Pin Pads has remained consistent for several months however this consistency depicts a higher than normal level and
requires further investigation into the reasons for failure. Actions are in place with the supplier, to report and monitor these failures.

The S92 CNIM problem is related to the increase in the number of base-unit calls, a problem which is currently being investigated. The reasons for fails
for base-units show a 25% increase due to Network fails, which relates to the increase in network calls and appears to have had an affect on the number
of visits related to Base Units. Please note that although the number of site visits made by engineers for base-units was high only around 66% involved
an actual swap, this is based on figures taken from D1 data rather than PowerHelp.

3.2.2 ONLINE NETWORK SERVICES
*All the graphs and tables within this section show all calls logged and do not include any call exclusions.

The HSD received a total of 2954 network calls during May 2006 with many resulting from the Fujitsu catalyst switch incident on 02 May 2006 and the
A&L firewall instability on the 25 May 2006. The catalyst incident also had an adverse impact on the daily network banking authorisation times which
had been exceptionally stable since December 2005. Following the incident several design improvements have been identified and are being
implemented under change control to reduce the likelihood of the situation reoccurring.

The ADSL fault volumes have remained consistent however we have seen an increase in ISDN and VSAT incidents. Specifically we have asked Cable &
Wireless to investigate with why Post Office is experiencing such high numbers of VSAT PES hardware replacements.

The introduction of backup network resilience has reduced the number of branches experiencing network unavailability at any given time. This has
particularly helped the branches that are with prolonged outages due to postmaster generated cease requests.

4.3 KEY CROSS DOMAIN PROBLEM UPDATE

The new RAG status for the key cross domain problems are based on the criteria —

Business Impact

BI score

Root Cause RC score

High (H) >20 outlets affected Root cause unknown, no progress or no workaround in place

Medium (M) >1 <20 outlets affected Root cause determined, implementation agreed/needs to be agreed or
workaround in place

Low (L) <1 outlet affected Fix implemented and problem being monitored

RAG status = BI score x RC score = (RED = 6-9) (AMBER = 3-6) (GREEN = 1-2)
The differentiator to be applied between a RED & AMBER RAG status score of 6 depends on whether the RC is known and whether a workaround is in
place i.e. if a the score of 6 is derived by a “Root cause unknown, no progress or no workaround in place = RC score of 3” then the RAG will be red.

If the RC score was derived by “Root cause determined, implementation agreed/needs to be agreed or a workaround in place = RC score of 2 then the

RAG will be amber.
