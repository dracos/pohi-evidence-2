2 MARCH REVIEW OF SERVICE

2.1 MANAGEMENT SUMMARY

Fujitu's service during March 2006 continued its high levels of service with all Service Levels being achieved with the exception of one Day J data
recovery. Of note however, is the considerable increase in hardware incidents logged by PostMasters - split across both Pin Pad and Ithaca counter
printers with approximately 450 extra incidents each. The engineer service performance remained above 95% compliance. The number of engineer visits
was 5855 against a 'target' of 3875. The Pin Pad increase was a direct result of the S90 counter release, whilst the Ithaca Counter printer increase is
currently under investigation and is the highest recorded monthly figure.

During February, there were 3 major incidents. There was minimal impact due to prioritisation of incidents.

On line service provision retained a high (over 85%) of incidents resolved within 24 hours and also a continued reduction in faults logged compared to
2005.

The Priority Engineering service commenced pilot during March, realigning call priorities appropriately based on a redefined matrix. To date, the pilot is
in line with expectations in terms of profile of incidents logged and there has been no known negative feedback as to its implementation. Full rollout is

planned for 20th April 2006.

Finally, the Service Director responsibility will be transferred to Naomi Elliott from myself on 24th April.

2.1.2 Availability and Problem Management

Summary:
Within the Fujitsu Services domain for March 2006 there was 3 Incidents treated as major incidents. There were no incidents affecting VIP sites. There
were 7 further service affecting incidents which were outside of the Fujitsu domain.

Major Incidents:

Wednesday 15th March: KMA service unavailable at start of day due to security key upgrade the previous evening. Only affecting KMA Key Refresh
and replacement of Gateway/Counter exchanges. Full service available from 10:30. 13 Key Refresh calls logged and completed at start of service. MI E-
0603150003 refers.

Tuesday 7th March: A power failure at the BANK site affected a switch ultimately resulting in a service outage to 3 BBPE (BAS Broadband PE) routers
at Telehouse, GB_TELHS1_R10-004, 005 and 006. The service impact was a lose of online services to 792 branches. Incident occurred at 16:50 and was
resolved by 19:20. MI E-06030708 12 refers.

Friday 10th March: Total VSAT Network loss due to internal malfunction of Hughes router HOT-SLIM-1 at Docklands . On-Line services impact to all
VSAT branches from 09:32 . Services restored between 09:48 and 10:45. Call MIE E-0603100209 refers.

Other incidents in Fujitsu Services domain:

Thursday 2nd March: CAPO Cards were declined on Pinpad’s at 87 Bureau pilot sites for S90. Calls started at 09:59, this was temporarily resolved by a
counter re-boot and service was restored by 14:30. The Bureau enabling Ref Data was reversed overnight to these pilot sites, enabling them to resume
normal operation. Calls E-0603020307 & 380 refer.

Incidents Outside the Fujitsu Domain:

Tuesday 14th March: DVLA outage with both Links from Bootle & Wigan down at 16:17. DVLA service was unavailable from Swansea. DVLA
resolved problem (IBM issue) and service restored at 19:45.

Thursday 16th March: Loss of the A&L service at 16:53 both from the Wigan & Bootle data centres, loss of all 4 PI’s. Call logged with POL SCT, A&L
undertaken planned works on A&L Firewall, did not inform POL. Call E-0603161005 refers.

Thursday 23rd March: DVLA counter issues due to inconsistency in prices for Motor Products coming from the DVLA when bar codes scanned.
Problem caused by DVLA not updating the new price changes immediately to start from the 23rd on their application, POA Horizon system was updated

2.1.3 OBC Branch Change Service Summary

March 2006 saw compliant achievement from Fujitsu Services OBC in delivering a performance within the OLA targets. A total of 260 changes were
received, 15 of these were cancelled changes. With the exclusion of ‘Closures’ 222 changes were undertaken. Fujitsu were responsible for 9 change
requests not being completed on the target date. For Fujitsu this represents a failure rate of 3.15% against the new 4% OLA minus Closures. It also
represents a failure rate of 3.67% against the original target of 5%. There were 2 line plant failures; this represents a failure rate of 2.86% against the old
4% target excluding Line Plant. There is a difference of -0.70% compared to February’s Target without Closures. Compared to the February calculations
a difference of -1.49% against the 5% target rate and -0.68% against the 4% target rate, excluding line plant.

Post Office Ltd were responsible for 14 change requests not being completed on the target date, which represents 5.71% against the 4% target, which
shows a difference against the February calculations of -3.09%.

Corrective Actions
Fujitsu continues to work closely with Cable & Wireless to minimise the materialisation of issues surrounding provision of communications. The new
‘Express ISDN Installation Service’ has recently become an option to further reduce lead times for provision.
A Mobile ‘retrofit’ is now underway to ensuresMetvites‘aresexchanged for the modified version, early indications are that the modified Mobile and the
new PPMB are more reliable then their predecessor. Additional PPMB’s are being supplied to sites which had previously been under delivered to.

2.1.6 Business Continuity

The EDS disaster recovery (test 24), the Generic Agent (test 7) and the Alliance & Leicester (test 21) Business Continuity procedural walk-throughs
were successfully conducted during March.

On Sunday Sth March the operational business continuity test for the Web Services, APOP, DVLA and PAF servers, (test 26) was successfully
performed.

Over the weekend of the 11th and 12th of March the Database Server operational test (test 3), which now incorporates the APOP database, was also
successfully completed.

On Wednesday 15" March the business continuity operational retest of the External Data Gateway was successfully performed, and on Thursday 16"

2.1.8 Horizon Service Desk and Complaints

With the exception of specific engineer or HSD incidents where our improvement action is detailed in the table below, complaints continue to be

received against two main areas of the service; the quality and timeliness of the engineer support and the availability of the system in relation to On Line
transactions.

Both areas are receiving considerable focus and attention within Fujitsu Services to ensure we continue to build upon the improvements we are making to
the service and we continue to work with Post Office to manage expectations within the live estate.

Engineer Support

The “Priority Service” pilot commenced on 20th March within the Fujitsu Services Midlands region. In re-aligning the priorities allocated to hardware
faults within the Branch, Fujitsu Services is aiming to provide a much higher quality service which targets those faults really impacting the Branch’s
ability to complete critical transactions, with less critical faults, ie. back office printer issues, being dealt with the next day. We are working closely with
Post Office to ensure that we have allocated the correct priority to those faults reported and we will be reviewing the results of the pilot with Post Office

2.1.9 Business Incident Management

There were 43 BIM incidents during March. This is 29 less than reported in February 2006.

Of these incidents, 12 were within the 8 hour response SLT which were relating to the ‘Network Banking State 4’ exception type.
31 BIM incidents were within the 5 day response SLT.

The reduction in the number of BIM incidents is mainly due to S90 counter upgrade, post which there has been no State 4 exception type incidents
raised.

2.1.10 Engineer Services — Site Visits By Engineer

There were over 5855 site visits undertaken by engineers in March which is more than 1000 above normal levels. That said, the success rate was again
very high and all service level targets were exceeded. In mid-March the new SLT Priority initiative was piloted. This resulted in an increase of calls
being categorised as A priority. Four hundred and fourteen visits were undertaken within the pilot region in the first two weeks.

3.2.2 ONLINE NETWORK SERVICES
*All the graphs and tables within this section show all calls logged and do not include any call exclusions.

The HSD received a total of 1633 network incidents in Mar-06 which is an improvement over the Feb-06 performance but is still above an optimum
historic position of around 1200 network incidents per month. Although the ADSL incidents are slightly high they are distributed evenly across the
month and were not significantly impacted by any particular incidents.

Analysis of the 203 online transaction incidents shows that they were mostly related to A&L domain firewall instability.
The branch network MTBF and MTFR measurements are consistent with previous month although the VSAT reliability was affected by the 4 x branch

Personal Earth Station (PES) failures. The number of ADSL related gateway reboots caused by a Windows NT authentication issues continues to be high
but we are optimistic that a work-around in S92 connection manager will reduce the volume of these incidents.

Service improvements such as the upgrade of the BBPE (BAS) router IOS version and configuration standardisation are going smoothly and we are
pleased with the increased stability of the Cable & Wireless infrastructure and the reduction in frequency and duration of major incidents.

4.3 KEY CROSS DOMAIN PROBLEM UPDATE
The new RAG status for the key cross domain problems are based on the criteria —
Business Impact Bl score Root Cause RC score
High (H) >20 outlets affected 3 Root cause unknown, no progress or no workaround in place 3
Medium (M) >1 <20 outlets affected 2 Root cause determined, implementation agreed/needs to be agreed or 2
workaround in place
Low (L) <1 outlet affected 1 Fix implemented and problem being monitored 1

RAG status = BI score x RC score = (RED = 6-9) (AMBER = 3-6) (GREEN = 1-2)
The differentiator to be applied between a RED & AMBER RAG status score of 6 depends on whether the RC is known and whether a workaround is in
place i.e. if a the score of 6 is derived by a “Root cause unknown, no progress or no workaround in place = RC score of 3” then the RAG will be red.

If the RC score was derived by “Root cause determined, implementation agreed/needs to be agreed or a workaround in place = RC score of 2 then the

RAG will be amber.
