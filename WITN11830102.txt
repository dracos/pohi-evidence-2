Dear Sir/Madam,
We act for Post Office Limited ("POL").

We write to notify you of POL’s role in several cases referred to the Court of Appeal ("CoA")
by the Criminal Cases Review Commission ("CCRC") and that there may be cases prosecuted
by the PPSNI or its predecessor in which the same issues that have been examined by the
CCRC may arise.

We understand that the basis of the referral to the CoA is the use of evidence derived from
Horizon, the computer system used to record all transactions in post offices since around
1999. The CCRC announced its decision to refer 39 cases to the CoA on 26 March 2020 (see
https://ccre.gov.uk/ccrc-to-refer-39-post-office-cases-on-abuse-of-process-argument/) and a

further eight cases on 3 June 2020 (see https://ccrce.gov.uk/the-ccre-refers-eight-more-post-
office-cases-for-appeal-bringing-total-to-47-so-far/).

In December 2019, POL settled civil litigation with a large group of former sub-postmasters
regarding the reliability of Horizon. The settlement was followed by the handing down of
the 'Horizon Issues Judgment' (Bates v Post Office [2019] EWHC 3408 (QB) (available at
https://www. judiciary.uk/wp-content/uploads/2019/12/bates-v-post-office-judgment.pdf)).

POL is now conducting a post-conviction disclosure exercise ("PCDE") in accordance with its
common law duties as a prosecutor as set out in R (Nunn) v Chief Constable of Suffolk Police
[2014] UKSC 37 and R v Gohil [2018] EWCA Crim 140. The PCDE is being conducted in
respect of all defendants convicted in private prosecutions brought by POL (or, prior to their
separation in 2012, by Royal Mail) between 1999-2015 involving evidence derived from
Horizon.

Though POL privately prosecuted many cases in England & Wales, our understanding is that
all prosecutions in Northern Ireland were brought by the PPSNI or its predecessor.

In case it assists the PPSNI, we attach a spreadsheet containing details of cases we have
identified in the course of conducting the PCDE, which appear to have been prosecuted by
the PPSNI or its predecessor and may have resulted in convictions.

The attached spreadsheet has been compiled from information held by POL in which it had
some involvement in the case, such as providing exhibits, statements and data to the police.
As POL was not the prosecutor of these cases, the information held is limited, and it is
possible that there are further Northern Ireland cases which are not recorded in the
attached spreadsheet. We will inform you of any further cases we might identify but our
records should not be considered complete or definitive.
