1 MANAGEMENT SUMMARY

Within the Fujitsu Services domain for Mar 08 there were no Major Incidents and 3 service-affecting incidents, 1
incident relating to a VIP site and 2 further service-affecting incidents outside the Fujitsu domain, detailed in the
incident overview below.

Branch and counter availability continue to remain above target at 99.80% and 99.55% respectively. The pilot of
the revised Engineering service targets has continued, and has seen significant improvements in all areas from
February, with only 1 of the SLT’s (other than subsequent response) not achieving or exceeding target.

The Pin Pad refresh button introduced in February and saw 46 incidents resolved at the helpdesk, that would
otherwise have required an engineering visit, significantly reducing the downtime of the counter or single
counter branch.

1 offsite build was completed allowing early recommencement of services to the affected branch which
otherwise would have to wait for a PSTN fault to be resolved by BT.

Preventative maintenance visits were commenced with 108 taking place in March. The expectation is that these
will reduce the number of incidents and provide an enhanced service to the Post Office. We are tracking incident
logging for these sites and should have some stats to report in April’s SRB.

340 OBC changes were delivered in the month, with 2 failures due to an administrative issue. This has now
been addressed.

Major Incidents: None

Other incidents in Fujitsu Monday 3" March. There were 3 incidents during the day of transaction

Services domain: failures to the A&L service on the “B” link to Wigan. Investigations into the
failures by C&W showed no significant outages. The failure continued at
09:13 on the Tuesday 4" and a decision was taken to force the agents over
to Bootle, so both the A&L links were working to Bootle. Both A&L links were
stable for the rest of the day.

Investigations continued with networks testing out the end to end Wigan
connectivity and it was found that a higher than expected level of errors
existed in the Leiceser-2 router at a low memory level. The router was
reloaded at the end of the working day. On the Wednesday both the A&L
links were working to the Bootle data centre allowing full testing to take place
again on the Wigan circuit. No errors were reported throughout the day and
the service was allowed to re-home so the A&L links reverted to normal for
the start of service on Thursday. The A&L service has run OK since this
failure on the B Link. Call 143333 refers

Friday 14 March. Failures occurred on the A&L service on both PI links.
The “A” link to Bootle failed from 13:57 to 13:58, affecting 169 transactions
with 117 failures. The “B” link to Wigan failed from 14:01 to 14:03 affecting
282 transactions with 159 failures. Investigations proved that the error
occurred within the C&W domain inside the A&L Leicester datacentre which,
although only lasting for 20 seconds, caused an outage of several minutes
within the on-line service. Call 163250 refers.

Tuesday 48" March. An IP Stream outage occurred at 10:03 to 10:15
affecting up to 350 branches without on-line services. Issue was due to a BT
issue at Ealing POP (Point of Presence). Call 167495 refers

VIP sites affected this month: Tuesday 41" March. VIP site Trafalgar Square 015010, key exchange failed
and required a new gateway. This caused a loss of service at the branch
from 08:52 to 14:50. Call 154553 refers.

Incidents that were outside Monday 17" March. Transaction failures at 18% were identified on the

the Fujitsu Domain: “Link” service from 13:35 to 14:06. Link reported that the cause was due to
issues within the Barclays banking service, retries were successful. Call
165943 refers.

Friday 28" March. Failures on the Link service across both PI links to Bootle
& Wigan, from 13:02 to 13:22. This caused 2200 transaction failure’s, issue

There was second failure on the Link service but only affecting the PI to
Bootle. This caused 4500 transactions to fail from 17:02 to 17:35. This was

3 Business Continuity

3.1 Business Continuity Testing

During the week ending 16" March a retest was successfully completed on the HNG-X Security Service
and the security elements of the HNG-X Data-centre fail-over, completing all planned exercises within the
HNG-x initial Business Continuity and Disaster Recovery walk-through.

The focus is now to complete all amendments to the individual HNG-X test scripts, and to formally issue
the revised documents for approval prior to initial use during HNG-X DR/BC weeks 2 to 5 later this year.

During the week ending 30" of March the Post Office Limited Northern Data-centre Disaster Recovery
operational test was performed. This was a partial success as the file transfers from Fujitsu Services to
CSC were performed successfully to the Post Office Limited disaster recovery datacentre, however there
was a failure with the POLFS print queue. Diagnostic data was captured during the test which indicates
that this failure is within the Fujitsu Services domain and further investigation is planned with the SAP
Basis and Network Operation teams; there is a target date of 30" April for completion.

During the week ending 6" of April the Service Desk operational test was performed. The Stevenage
based Service Desk operations were relocated to the standby facilities in BRAO1, and the test was
successfully completed.

3.2 Forthcoming Testing

Awalk-through is being planned for the Third Line Support Service (SSC).

3.3 Business Continuity Planning

It is planned that all scripts reviewed during the HNG-X initial walk-throughs will be updated with the
changes identified and then formally issued for review and approval prior to initial use during HNG-X
DR/BC weeks 2 to 5 later this year.

Further work is required on writing the HNG-X Service and Support Services Business Continuity Plans.

3.4 Business Continuity Incidents and Issues
There were no Major Business Continuity Incidents or issues during March.

5.3. Branch Network Services / Central Network

5.3.1. Branch Network Services

During March 1586 calls were logged across all Network Types. This is an increase of 171 calls from February
with a BT outage causing at least 315 sites to lose comms as the main reason for this increase.

In total 40 calls failed to hit the 4 hour priority SLA. This gave 97.5% SLT conformance against the 95% target.
Of the 40 fails however, 70% occurred within the helpdesk or engineering domain. Those incidents are being
investigated by the corresponding Service and HSD managers, who are currently capturing figures and working
on Service Improvement Plans.

The Branch Network Services Troubleshooting Guide is currently in review and will be released to the helpdesk
and engineers imminently.

The IPMAX upgrade commenced at the end of January and completed in March. A number of calls were
suspended and could not be sent for BT Wholesale investigation during the 10-day bedding-in period. Whilst the
upgrade was ongoing, this resulted in a peak in volume and average age of intermittent network calls. Since
completion, however, call levels have reduced, and we expect to see reduced levels of intermittent faults in the
BT domain in the coming months as a result of the IPMAX upgrade.

One of our combined aims is to reduce the cost to POL of providing GSM for online service faults at branches.
The Returning GSM Units process has been reviewed and a new process and script for the help desk is being
looked into, which will allow us better control of the number of GSM units in the estate as well as reducing
overall cost where Postmasters have been using GSM longer than necessary when suffering minor intermittent
failures.

5.3.2 Central Network

The Access Layers for ADSL (IP Stream), ISDN (Metered) and VSAT branches remained stable during the
month with just one brief and small outage in the BT Wholesale’s ADSL domain. Wigan and Bootle datacentre
LANs were also extremely stable. However there were two transaction affecting incidents that impacted the
Fujitsu and C&W equipment hosted at A&L Carlton Park in Leicester datacentres.

7 Sense & Respond

The data captured during March has highlighted the top 2 highest demand types as being Hardware and
Software. Trend analysis has shown the total call volume for Hardware during March to be 7690 and Software
calls to be 2188.

There was a total of 1302 Pin Pad calls during March- an increase of 174 calls from February. HSD front-line
have been advised to check Tivoli events for all Pin Pad calls received to avoid any unnecessary HSD
diagnostics/engineer visits. The recent addition of a data table refresh button to Horizon has allowed HSD to
resolve more calls, and they now resolve 46% of calls: a 5% increase on February and a 7% increase on
January.

For hardware demand, the highest call volume received is for Counter Printer faults (2583 calls received in
March — a decrease of 30 calls from February). Approximately 42% of calls are resolved by adjusting the
consumables or by doing a self-clean cycle, and an IVR is being considered which will enable PMs to carry out
these procedures without having to speak to an agent. The improved ink cartridge which is being distributed
throughout the estate should also help to reduce the number of counter printer calls.

The most commonly used repair code for Base Unit calls continues to be ‘Unit replacement’, followed by ‘user
advised / no action taken.’ The call volume from February to March has reduced by 70 calls (10% of February's
call volume), but overall performance has been stable, with FMS resolving 84% of calls in February and 87% of
calls in March.

1194 calls were logged during March as a monitor fault (-53 calls from February). This represented a total of 881
faults (74%) being resolved by engineering and 313 faults (26%) by the HSD. A meeting is being planned
between HSD, Service Delivery and Optima, with the aim of improving diagnostics the desk fix rate.

During March, 4435 chase calls were received, with the highest chase call types being for Engineering Updates
(1817) and Customer Calls (1751 calls).

After tracking Inappropriate calls data throughout March, the calls received at the HSD totalled at 1405 (10% of
all calls), a slight decrease from February. HSD has highlighted all the sites that have made 3 or more
Inappropriate calls for the month of March and passed this information to POL. Outgoing coaching calls are to
be provided by HSD to branches who have made multiple inappropriate calls, with the aim of identifying any
outstanding issues at those Post Offices and also coaching the PMs in the differences between the Horizon and
NBSC desks.
