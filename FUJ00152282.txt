1 MANAGEMENT SUMMARY

Within the Fujitsu Services domain for Feb 08 there were no Major Incidents and 2 service-affecting incidents,
no incidents relating to VIP sites and 2 further service-affecting incidents outside the Fujitsu domain, detailed in
the incident overview below.

Branch and counter availability continue to remain above target at 99.78% and 99.53% respectively. The pilot of
the revised Engineering service targets has continued, and has seen improvements in all areas from January,
despite the challenges faced due to the SMC having to invoke DR and relocate to Bracknell, meaning that Base
Unit builds could not be started and the following day HSD and SMC being evacuated for 2 hours due to a gas
leak at a neighbouring building, again preventing new Base Unit builds from being started.

The Pin Pad refresh button was introduced on the 26" February and saw 22 incidents resolved at the helpdesk,
in the remaining 3.5 days of the month, that would otherwise have required an engineering visit, significantly
reducing the downtime of the counter or single counter branch.

3 offsite builds were completed allowing early recommencement of services to the affected branches, that
otherwise would have to wait for a PSTN fault to be resolved by BT.

Preventative maintenance visits were commenced with 22 taking place the last 10 days of February. The
expectation is that these will reduce the number of incidents and provide an enhanced service to the Post
Office.

313 OBC changes were delivered in the month, with no failures.

2 incidents failed the Post Shops SLT, which is most disappointing. This has been pinpointed to one help desk
agent, and the agent has been retrained for this process. An additional monitoring process has been put in
place with IMT to ensure that all Post Shop Incidents are followed through from there.

The overall number of complaints logged with NBSC has increased in February to 94. This is as expected as all
complaints are now being passed through to Fujitsu Services, rather than for information only. We are
identifying more areas of improvement and are able to take this to a greater level of detail, i.e. agent level, if
required. There are a number of activities taking place to drive this number, particularly around process and
ETA’s.

No branches failed the Day J targets.

Significant activities from the Service Improvement Plan are included in this review book. A number of the
branch improvements have been in pilot and demonstrated positive results, and these will be rolled out in
agreement with POL over the coming weeks.

3 Business Continuity

3.1 Business Continuity Testing

Throughout the week commencing 11" of February the initial HNG-X business continuity walk-throughs were
conducted using the operational business continuity test scripts being developed for HNG-X. In general the
exercise was successfully conducted. A report has been produced detailing the actions identified during the
week. It was concluded that major improvements could be implemented within the Security Service script and
the next draft version has been produced. A follow-up walk-through has been planned for the 12” of March
when a further review will be conducted on the revised HNG-X Security Service and Data-centre fail-over
operational test scripts.

On the 7” of February the loss of both of the network links, between the SMC in Stevenage (STE04) and Wigan
and Bootle Data-centres, (one required a visit from BT and the other link was misconfigured) meant that the
SMC were unable to monitor the live estate. The decision was taken to invoke disaster recovery for the SMC.
who relocated to BRAO1. This live invocation has been documented as it verified the SMC preparedness for
disaster recovery.

Fujitsu Services Network Operations Centre, for shared network infrastructure, successfully conducted a
relocation test of the NOC operations on Saturday 2™ of February. This test has been documented and will be
incorporated within the RMGA Business Continuity 2008 Test Report.

3.2 Forthcoming Testing

On the 12" of March a further review shall be conducted on the revised HNG-X Security Service and Data-
centre fail-over operational test scripts.

The Post Office Limited Northern Data-centre operational test is planned for the 26-29" of March.
Awalk-through is being planned for the Third Line Support Service (SSC).

3.3 Business Continuity Planning

It is planned that all scripts reviewed during the HNG-X initial walk-through will be updated with the changes
identified.

Further work is required on the HNG-X service and Support Services Business Continuity Plans.

3.4 Business Continuity Incidents and Issues

Operations at STE04 where disrupted on two occasions during February, firstly on Thursday the 7" of February
when the SMC relocated to BRA01, and secondly on the 8" of February when a major gas leak resulted in all
staff being evacuated from STE04. The gas leak was repaired in a timely manner and HSD and SMC staff were
not required to relocate to BRAO1.

5.3. Branch Network Services / Central Network

5.3.1. Branch Network Services

1415 were calls logged across all Network Types into the HSD during February. This is an increase of 400 calls
from January, with the main cause of this increase being a BT outage which caused at least 214 sites to lose
online services.

In total 42 calls failed to hit the 4 hour priority SLA. This gave 97% SLT conformance against the 95% target. Of
the 42 fails, however, 83% occurred within the helpdesk or engineering domain. Those incidents are now being
investigated by the HSD and Engineering Service Managers with a view to capturing any improvements that can
be made; this will result in an even better service being provided to the customer.

The Branch Network Services Troubleshooting Guide is currently in review and will be available for release
imminently.

The IPMAX upgrade began at the end of January and was in full swing in February. A number of calls were
suspended and could not be sent for BT Wholesale investigation during the 10-day bedding in period. This
resulted in a higher than usual volume and average age of intermittent network calls but this is expected to

reduce once the upgrades have been completed in March.

One of the key achievements in February was the resolution of two long-standing calls: BT rewired Cemetary
Road’s ADSL line, and an ADSL re-provide was implemented at Rufford Drive. The overall effect of closing
down these oldest calls was reflected in the reduction of the average age of Solid Down calls from 35 days at
the start of the month to 22 days by the end of the month.

One of our combined aims is to reduce the cost to POL of providing GSM for online service faults at branches.
As part of that process, we have tightened up our procedures in both distribution and returning of GSM devices.
This will ultimately allow us better control of the use of the solution, by controlling the number of GSM devices in
the field as well as by preventing Post Masters from using GSM longer than necessary when suffering minor
intermittent failures. Parameters have been put into place for escalations and the Comms Management Team
have obtained a supply of spare returns envelopes which they can distribute to Postmasters who have mislaid
the original returns packaging.

5.3.2 Central Network

The Access Layers for ADSL (IP Stream), ISDN (Metered) and VSAT branches remained extremely stable. The
ATM node incident at Llandudno & Swansea affected multiple BT Wholesale broadband customers and was in
line with the expected characteristics of BT's network design. However due to political sensitivity BT Wholesale
did not raise an MSO outage notification to confirm the incident. The missing MSO along with the extended
outage duration has been raised by Fujitsu in our service reviews with BT Wholesale.

7 Sense & Respond

The data captured during February highlighted the top two demand types as Hardware and Software. Trend
analysis has shown the total call volume for Hardware during February to have decreased by 565 calls (total of
8030) and a decrease of Software calls by 88 calls (total of 2075).

For hardware demand, the highest call volume received is for Counter Printer faults (2613 calls received in
February- a decrease of 306 calls from January). A redesigned ink cartridge is being distributed by
Communisys, which should result in a reduction in ‘Print quality’ and ‘Consumables issue’ call types- which are
currently the cause of 1300 calls per month.

1247 calls were logged during February as a monitor fault (+166 calls from January). This represented a total of
960 faults (77%) being resolved by engineering and 287 faults (23%) by the HSD. A ‘Sharing Knowledge’
meeting is being organised between Service Delivery, HSD and Optima, in order to review HSD KELs and
identify further checks which could help to improve the HSD fix rate.

1128 calls were received for Pin Pad errors during February. The Pin Pad Card Cleaner project has now been
signed off and is being planned. A Pin Pad data refresh button has been added to the estate, and it is
anticipated that this will resolve many problems which could not previously be resolved by the helpdesk.

A new Sense and Respond project has been undertaken to look into the base unit calls resolved by the HSD
and by an engineer visit. During the coming months we will track the number of calls logged and the number
resolved by HSD/Engineering as the primary measure. The data will then be broken down into call severity, fault
types and resolution. This will be analysed for trends/errors and will enable HSD to identify calls which have
been incorrectly sent to Engineering. This will enable us to ‘shift to the left’, reducing the length of time that the
fault is outstanding and improving counter availability.

During February, 1967 customer chase calls were received (+565 calls from January). Agents are being advised
to set expectations clearly with the PM.

After tracking Inappropriate calls data throughout February, 1417 calls were received at the HSD, a slight
decrease from January. This represents 10% of all calls received at the Helpdesk.

HSD has highlighted all the sites that have made 3 or more Inappropriate calls for the months of January and
February which has been passed direct to POL. This information is to be compared with Inappropriate calls
recorded by NBSC to identify any trends. HSD is to proactively contact those repeat callers to offer support.
