Closing Submissions

My Lord, if I may I propose to keep these closing submissions relatively brief by
adopting what I said in my skeleton argument and in opening and by simply

highlighting the key points in the argument and the evidence.

The starting point must be a consideration of the everyday factual position where a
business, the principal, employs an agent to handle its stock and collect payments for
it, and retains the agent to prepare figures showing what transactions have been

performed, for what price, and what the outstanding balance is between the parties.

The agent is paid to conduct this business and prepare the financial information, and
as in this case, he may even be remunerated on the basis of the amount of business

shown as transacted.

It is plainly important for the business to be confident in the figures and in part that is
why the agent is contractually obliged to prepare them accurately, since that then
requires the agent to allege his own breach of contract in order even begin to suggest

that his own figures are wrong.

The agent is also paid to keep stock and cash safe and secure. He is entrusted with
that cash and stock on the basis that he will, to use the same word, account for it.
That means that it is for the agent to produce the cash and stock, or what now

represents the cash and stock, when required. If the agent does not produce the stock
