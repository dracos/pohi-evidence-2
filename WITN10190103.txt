POST OFFICE LIMITED - REMUNERATION PACKAGE FOR NEW CHIEF
FINANCE OFFICER

I am writing to seek your approval of the proposed remuneration package for a new
CFO for Post Office Limited (POL).

The POL Board has advised me that it needs to expand the CFO job remit to
accelerate the delivery of the company’s strategy, which as you are aware is
significantly funded by Government. The Board consider that, while the current
CFO, Chris Day, has done well in terms of the remit originally given to him, which
was primarily around the separation of the company from Royal Mail, he is unable to
carry out the revised role as he lacks the necessary breadth of experience.

POL wants a CFO with significantly more commercial experience, particularly in
leading challenging business turnarounds. POL faces significant strategic
challenges which require it to cut costs aggressively and diversify income. For
example, competitor networks for parcel pick up/ drop off are now bigger than POL’s,
Government contracts are increasingly competitively priced, and revenues continue
to be eroded by digitisation.

Following a comprehensive recruitment process involving 70 potential candidates,
they have identified Alisdair Cameron. He is at present working as Director of
Energy Transition at Centrica plc leading the transition of British Gas’s Energy
businesses to embrace Ofgem’s new Standards of Conduct. He was previously MD
for British Gas Enterprise, with 700,000 sites, and turned it around from loss-making
to stability. As Finance Director for British Gas, a £14bn turnover business with 22m
customers, he was at the heart of the leadership team that transformed this business
from relatively low profits of £93m p.a. in 2005/6 to a sustainable £623m over
2010/12. This included operating cost reductions of £500m, at the same time
improving service levels from the worst to amongst the leading companies in the
sector.

He has therefore completed complex commercial negotiations and completed
significant cost transformation activities in a highly regulated and sensitive political
environment. We are satisfied that he is a high quality candidate with the right track
record to drive POL’s strategy.

To secure him, POL wishes to offer a remuneration package that is higher than the
existing CFO's. This reflects the increased responsibilities of the post and the need
to offer a package that will be able to secure him.

By accepting the POL post, Mr Cameron will be taking a pay cut. At Centrica, his
potential maximum remuneration i /excluding share options.

5. Question: “How does pay compare to the third sector?”
In summary:

a. POL faces many strategic challenges that are common to very commercial businesses, not
least the level of competition which it faces and the level of organisational change being
implemented. We don’t therefore consider that POL comfortably sits within the third
sector; and

b. Given POL does not have a direct comparator to benchmark against, they therefore looked
at a range of organisations, including ShEx companies, mutuals and FTSE listed companies to
establish a median level of pay.

a. POL’s challenges and the third sector

POL is in a unique position. Whilst there are other Government owned companies in existence, very
few if any are facing the many significant strategic challenges which POL needs to address, whilst at
the same time modernising and transforming.

In terms of challenges, first and foremost is competition:

© For the first time, competitor networks for parcel pick up/ drop off are now bigger than
POL’s. Rival Collect+ has publically stated its intention to target POL, and POL must respond

© POL's bill payment business has essentially been taken by competitors such as Paypoint and
others. They must not let this happen to mails, which makes up over 40% of its business

It must compete for Government contracts, which are increasingly competitively priced
meaning POL must cut its costs further. And Government business has simply not been as
forthcoming as anticipated, providing further downward pressure on revenue.

e  Inresponse POL must diversify in to other markets like financial services, which is again very
competitive given the established players

e It continues to compete with alternative channels / digital evolution (e.g. the removal of the
car tax later this year removes significant levels of revenue)

The other significant challenge is the level of modernisation it must undertake:

e It has already transformed more of its outlets than the whole of Barclays network, but it
has more than that amount still to do

e It must cut significant costs out of its central functions — they are aiming for over 25% - to
have a chance of reducing the Network Subsidy Payments to the levels Govt aspires to
(from current £160m to c£50m from 2018)

© POLis still emerging from under Royal Mail’s shadow, post separation 2 years ago,
increasing the challenge culturally and organisationally

e Atthe same time, POL must continue to meet customer, subpostmaster, staff and other
stakeholder engagement and satisfaction levels, across a network bigger than all the banks
combined, given it remains in the public eye.

POL, and ShEx, therefore consider the challenge for the incoming CFO to be very different from a
typical third sector or government owned business — driving down costs very aggressively,
renegotiating contracts, overseeing the development of the new financial services business,
amongst others.
