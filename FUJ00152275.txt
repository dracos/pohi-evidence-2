1 MANAGEMENT SUMMARY

Summary: Within the Fujitsu Services domain for Aug 07 there were no Major Incidents and 2 service affecting
incidents, no incidents relating to VIP sites and two further service affecting incidents outside the Fujitsu
domain. Details are given in the Incident Overview below.

Branch and counter availability continue to remain above target at 99.8% and 99.5% respectively. Service levels
on A & C priority engineering calls are still failing but remain steady and minimising the direct impact to the
branch remains a priority. The revised proposal for Engineering SLTs is now tabled and under discussion, with
the intent to agree this with all parties during September.

89 OBC changes were delivered in the month, with zero combined failures. Work continues to continually
improve the pre-opening testing to further reduce the risk of early life incidents.

The Epson Counter Printer rollout continues to programme, and the printers with the new cartridges are
showing very low call volumes. Planning is under discussion to agree how and when to introduce these to the
whole estate tom ensure this benefit is realised as quickly as practical.

The revised complaints process is working well. The total number of complaints received this month was down,
with an overall total of 86, with 79 passed to Fujitsu for investigation, with 5 deemed justified. This is a great
improvement over last month, and confirms the progress made on communication with PMs, in particular on
managing ETAs.

Significant activities from the Service Improvement Plan are included in this review book. All are tracking well to
plan, and many are showing immediate benefits in areas such as network call referrals, improving remote fixes
and giving engineers wider diagnostics capability to improve first time fix rate.

4.2 Engineering Service

August 07 has seen call volumes drop to less than 5000 calls, a drop of 925 from August last year. There were
702 A priority calls in this period.

With just over 70% of the live estate branches now with Epson counter printers, we have seen a continued drop
in Ithaca calls. Epson and Ithaca calls combined are down by 511 which is a major reason for the fall in
engineering calls.

Compared to July, August has seen a slight improvement in A and C priority conformance. The A priority calls
fixed within four hours rose from 71.9% to 74.5% and the six hour from 82.4% to 82.6%. Conformance on C
priority next day calls showed final figures of 91.0%, an increase of 1.2% on the previous month which came in at
89.8%. Next Day +2 hours on C priorities reached 91.9% conformance after being 90.7% in June.

The non-conformant SLT performance can be partially attributed to certain regions where there were some
challenges in the field. This resulted in a large number of carry over failed calls and slightly higher than normal
WIP which impacted the subsequent day’s performance. To respond to these issues, heads were re-positioned
around the country, and extra resource was scheduled including during the weekend. The strain on the desk
resulted in an adverse effect on their performance in prioritising calls as well as their long term training plans.
This was compounded by desk resilience challenges that have been addressed. Fujitsu assisted our engineering
supplier through review of their productivity models to ensure engineer resource was utilised most efficiently.
Where possible, Fujitsu is prepared to provide engineers to assist in the work driving down the WIP. We continue
to work proactively together on a daily basis to ensure the desired focus remains on the oldest calls in order to
drive down the WIP. This includes effective utilisation of Saturdays to ensure we start the next week on the right
foot. We are already seeing an improvement, with the WIP consistently below 300.

There were also challenges with the call management system OTI link. SLT performance would not have been
directly affected due to manual voicing of C priority calls. However, this additional work would have created an
additional strain on the engineering and HSD desk.

The automatic SLA expiry time generator had to be turned off due to a possible link to OTI failure. This meant that
we had to revert to the previous generator that suffers from inaccuracies in the RDMC. This would have
understandably contributed to some of the failed calls. The Auto SLA macro will be re-introduced in September
with TFS.

With the appreciated co-operation of Caerphilly Post Office, we were able to perform an off site build for
Pontyprydd to enable them to trade over GSM solution whilst the communications issue was resolved.

180 ADSL “diagnostic” modems have been purchased and distributed to engineers for use at branches with
communication issues. They have been well received, and are already reducing the number of base unit swaps
to diagnose a communications fault.

A hardware call reduction plan is in place to reduce calls logged at HSD and hence passed to engineering. There
are nine key areas that are being focused on in order to refine efforts on the predicted big wins. Examples are the
addition of a Horizon button to reload Pin Pad reference data, and the ‘blind’ reboot process.

Fujitsu has continued to deliver additional POA training to our engineer service help desk. The weekly reviews of
failure points will continue and feed into the Service Improvement Plan. Every engineer now has a base unit /
peripheral handbook. This will compliment their initial training.

Calls logged by branches which resulted in an on-site visit from an engineer were down on August 2006 despite
the overall increase in incoming hardware calls to HSD. August has again seen all counter availability targets met
with branch availability at 99.79% and counter availability at 99.47%. This equates to 30 out of 13993 branches
and 182 out of 34351 counters being unavailable during the month, an improvement on July.

4.3. Branch Network Services / Central Network

4.3.1. Branch Network Services
During August there were 983 calls logged across all Network Types into the HSD.

SLTs

The ‘Priority A— 6 hours’ SLT saw 30 failures from a total of 983 closed calls measured, resulting in 96.6%
achievement. The majority of SLT failures have been attributed to delays in call handling at the service desks,
this is being taken up with the Service Desk with improvements expected.

Other issues in August

As described with 4.3.2 Central Network. BT made the required change to the carrier service on the VSAT
network. This has improved the overall VSAT service with fewer transaction failures being recorded by each
Branch.

August saw a reduction in Network Calls being received with a reduction of 9% on the previous month. This can
be attributed to a prolonged period of sustained weather conditions in addition to a reduction in repeat faults.

We have also implemented a new fault resolution process. We are happy to say that the new process is working
well and is allowing us to diagnose faults in more detail. This has allowed us to pinpoint the failure to a single
resolution owner which in turn aids quicker resolution. Since implementing the new process call volume has
reduced and we expect to see a similar trend in call to fix times over the coming months.

4.3.2 Central Network

This month the ISDN and Central Networks were extremely stable. The DSL network had a small blip in the BT
Wholesale domain which caused brief packet loss affecting most of their domestic and business customers but
following the loss of packets being processed by the node the resilience performed as per design. A change was
implemented at the Turin SkyPark that has lead to better VSAT stability (lower packet loss and less frequent
outages).

4.6 Data Centre Operations Service / Data File Transfer

There were no major incidents in relation to the Data Centre Operations Service within the Data Centre central
Infrastructure for the month of August 07.

6 Sense & Respond

Due to the timescales around production of the Sense & Respond data and analysis, the information in the SRB
will always be for the previous month. The full Sense & Respond report is issued separately.

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY:

The data captured during July has highlighted the top 2 highest demand types as being Hardware and Software.
Trend analysis has shown an increase in total call volume for Hardware (+139) and a slight decrease for
Software calls (-18) from June to July. During July, the HSD fixed 67% of calls received (12156) and this
represented an increase of 2% of total calls fixed on the HSD from June.

For networking calls, a focus has been made to track ADSL and ISDN call volumes and the mean time to clear.
Trending has highlighted a slight increase in call volume from June to July for ASDL calls (+59) however a
decrease of ISDN calls (-160). The mean time to clear for ASDL calls has decreased by 26 hours however there
has been a slight increase in the mean time to clear for ISDN calls (+126 hours).

For Pin Pad errors, the volume of Pin Pad calls for July totalled at 1390 and the volume of FMS Pin Pad swaps
for July was 692. HSD will be monitoring Pin Pad calls on a monthly basis and where the branch has a Site

9 and to also establish common user errors on their account.

For hardware demand, the highest call volume received was Counter Printer faults (just below 5000 calls
received in July). In July, 20% of frontline fixes were resolved by turning the counter printer offfon. Epson have
initiated a re-design of the RED ink cartridge in an attempt to resolve the “Printing blank” issue, this will only be
for the units that are using the Pigment based ink.

A trail of the redesigned ink will start on the 01/08/07 and is expected to run for 2 months.

Another area of focus is regarding chase/repeat calls. July has seen an overall increase in repeat calls of 463
calls compared to the month of June. A99 calls have seen increases in Base units by 88 and Online services by
70 calls, however, the C45 repeat calls show the biggest increase in repeat calls with an overall increase of
approx 348.

An additional objective regarding engineering telephony call volume is to reduce the number to the HSD from
Touch, Fujitsu and ROMEC engineers. The Call volume for the HSD has increased during July by 107 calls
whereas CMT have experienced a slight decrease (99 calls). The volumes will be monitored during August to see
if this trend continues and further investigation will be conducted where appropriate.

For July, a new project has been initiated, focusing on monitor faults. The objective is to reduce the end to end
process time for resolving monitor faults and to work towards reducing the total monitor calls volumes into the
HSD. 1032 calls were logged during July as a monitor fault of which 30% were fixed by the HSD and 68%
resolved by engineers.

A project monitoring Inappropriate calls between the HSD and NBSC has achieved is objective of reducing the
number of calls between the desks from 10% to 5%. The team will continue to monitor the inappropriate call
volumes and ensure monthly communication is maintained between the HSD and NBSC desks.
