Dear Mr Bailey

Your letter of 11 March 2011 tendering your resignation refers. As you are aware I have acknowledged
your resignation separately. My purpose in writing to you today is to address the other points that you
raised in your letter. To help in this matter I have enclosed a copy of the Subpostmaster Contract for
reference.

I am sorry that you feel that Post Office Ltd has failed in its duty of care to you as a Subpostmaster
following the suspension of your contract on 5 January 2011; however in dealing with your case I have
followed due process which has been agreed with the National Federation of Subpostmasters.

You appear to be under the impression that you are an employee and as such have certain rights, however
your Subpostmaster contract with Post Office Ltd is a contract for services and consequently you are an
agent, not an employee (Section 1 paragraph 11 refers).

Under the terms of your contract for services you are not obliged to render personal service and as such
you can arrange to absent yourself from your branch provided you make suitable arrangements for the
continuation of customer service in your absence. As Subpostmaster you are not entitled to annual leave
but, subject to certain conditions, you may be able claim for reimbursement of the necessary cost of your
substitution when taking a holiday (Section 4 refers). Our records show that the last claim made as
Subpostmaster at Post Office® Howey branch was in August 2009 and you have not claimed for holiday
substitution since and therefore there are no outstanding claims.

Again in accordance with the contract for services when a Subpostmaster is suspended, his remuneration in
respect of any period of suspension is withheld as long as the suspension continues (Section 19 paragraph £
refers). However your suspension was on 5" January 2011 and you have received the remuneration for
these five days in your January 2011 remuneration advice totalling £270.83 net.
