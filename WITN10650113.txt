Tony I've had a very fast canter through.

Some disjointed and very preliminary thoughts (without having gone back to the original judgment or to my Note or your
skeleton).

It's an exhaustive and exhausting judgment, and the first 70 paragraphs on the law are like a first term law student's
essay, but one has to be forgiving as, right or wrong, he is under serious professional attack, and he has dug deep to try
and justify each and every finding.

The problem we now face arises from two bad decisions of his - first not to shut out irrelevant evidence and second not to
control himself to making only necessary findings, but we can probably only complain about the second.

Overall, this judgment is a bit of a curate's egg.

Some silly points - eg the waiver argument and the point that he made some findings in favour of the PO.

On the other hand, some of his points have force - especially our cross-examining and/or making submissions on some
of the issues we now complain about his making findings, and, possibly, in relation to one or two facts which are relevant
to the interpretation issues.

My feeling is that he has an arguable defence on some of the findings we complain about, but not on others, but how
many of each and how good his defence is on the ones where he has a defence, I have not had the time to study
properly or think about.
