1 Introduction

This is a summary report addressed to ICL Pathway and Horizon management to comment on
the conduct of the Model Office Rehearsal 3 phase of ICL Pathway’s Release 2 testing. It
focuses on the testing environment, the testing activities performed, the results of these, and
the conclusions drawn.

2 Scope and Objectives

MOR3 was the last of three planned Model Office Rehearsals. It’s major objectives were to
prove:

the final draft of the operating processes and procedures across all domains
ICL Pathway’s target solution for architecture, network and connectivity
ICL Pathway’s supporting infrastructure

and reiterate the migration process from Release Ic to R2

that supporting and reporting processes and routes are in place

Tt was not a purpose of MOR3 to perform R2 functionality proving although note was taken,
and incidents raised, in response to errors found. It was expected that all available counter
tests would be executed to some degree.

The format of the testing was identical to that used both previous rehearsal cycles.

The MOR3 cycle continued to use the Model Office site based in Borough High Street and
was supported by ICL Pathway operations at the R2 target live operational environment in the
Bootle data centre.

3. System under test

3.1. Environment

The test environment within the ICL Pathway domain comprised two data centres, one at
release 1c and the other the target R2 go live solution, with links to all external systems. Full
details of these are given in Appendix A.

The test environment within the Horizon domain comprised seven Post Offices; three of these
offices were migrated from release 1c to R2; four new offices were introduced into the R2
system, two from ECCO and two that were previously manual. A new addition for this pass
was the introduction of the Pentium II 400mhz processors.

Gateway PC’s, for the transmission of data to remote sites, were located at Farnborough,
Huthwaite and De La Rue, Tewkesbury.

4 Test Conduct

4.1 Test Phases

The testing was performed by Horizon test staff and Post Office counter staff over the period
22" October to 12" November inclusive, the stages being:

m 22° _ 24' October, phase 1 of migration, moving three outlets
from release 1c to R2, with the data centre at release 1c. The start
of MOR3 was delayed by 1 day due to a time synchronisation
problem across all domains.

m 25"~ 26" October, data centre migration to R2.

@ 27° October - 12 November, full R2 operation and the
introduction of new R2 outlets. The day lost at the beginning of the
cycle was not recovered using planned contingency. This resulted
in test execution completing 1 day behind the original schedule

All relevant data suppliers and receivers participated in this phase of testing i.e. TIP, CAPS.

Conclusion
5.1. Summary

MOR3 proved most of the test phase objectives.

Of the 12 limitations marked as cleared in the MOR3 Release Notice;
e Seven were successfully tested, confirming clearance

e Three were not covered by the MO test plan

e Two limitations remained;

e APS - production of receipt following printer fault. The noted limitation was still in
evidence

e Training Mode - Although the new processors should have resolved the difficulties
regarding this area, training mode was not available during MOR3.

The content of the counter procedures has stabilised somewhat since the first cycle. The
presentation remained at draft standard (full production standard procedures are not expected
until MOT).

ICL Pathway’s architecture, networks, connectivity and supporting infrastructure moved
closer to the target solution.

There are a number of outstanding incidents. Details of these, as at 21‘' December 1998, can
be found in Appendix 2.

5.3 Issues

The following issues were identified:

@ The initial set up of the TPS system caused a number of problems
with the dates of the files being sent to TIP.

@ The initial set up of the CAPS data caused some initial problems
with payments.

™@ On the first day of testing the date was set incorrectly on the
counters. This caused a one day delay in the testing.

@ In general, the migration tools performed well, however some
minor problems were identified. These will be addressed by
software and reference data changes in parallel to MOT testing.

@ Most of the printed output of the system was factually incorrect,
not produced or did not conform to specification.

@ Of the 19 cash accounts produced, only one balanced account was
achieved (which contained a single days transactions)

@ Although all of the planned changes to reference data were applied
successfully at the counter, 3 of the 6 planned changes created a
imbalance in subsequent Cash Accounts

Training mode was unavailable
Carded Casual Agent functionality remained unavailable

A foreign encashment could not be made on a Rel 2 counter if the
customers nominated office was previously set as Rel Ic

@ The foreign encashment count proved unreliable during the
migration period

@ A duplicate encashment was allowed via the HSHD (it should be
noted that the original transaction was performed on a Rel Ic
counter)

Late in the cycle, amendment of the Back-Office printer settings
resulted in a vast improvement in production quality and times. To
improve this instance, the affected printers should be correctly
configured during the installation process.

ITM discrepancies occurred between Pathway & TIP due to
Pathway deriving their ITMs from the Menu Hierarchy & TIP
deriving theirs from ITMs held on POCL Reference Data, resulting
in file rejections. This issue is being addressed, in the short term,
by TIP removing this part of their file validation.

TIP reject files containing ITM errors on the first ITM error found
and Pathway are not notified of any other errors or further
occurrences of the error until the file is re-sent

Organisational Unit Version numbers differed between TIP &
Pathway due to the timing of the incremental reference data drops,
resulting in file rejections. This issue is being addressed by TIP
removing this part of their file validation.

FTMS was not used for the automatic transfer of files to remote
sites.
