1 MANAGEMENT SUMMARY

Summary: Within the Fujitsu Services domain for May 07 there was 1 Major Incident, three service affecting
incidents within the Fujitsu domain, no incidents relating to VIP sites and two further service affecting incidents
outside the Fujitsu domain. Details are given in the Incident Overview below.

The major incident occurred on 3% May: - An IP stream failure occurred, due to a router memory fault. Branches
switched to the other router and caused an overload on the second router later in the day. Up to 1921 branches
were affected. Corrective action work took place overnight to upgrade the memory in both routers.

Branch and counter availability continue to remain above target at 99.7% and 99.4% respectively. Daily tracker
performance remains good, and the weekly dashboard continues to assist in identifying specific service issues.
The management of intermittent faults remains a joint priority. 80 OBC changes were delivered in the month
and all the OBC targets were exceeded with all OLAs met.

Incoming calls to the desk increased marginally this month, but no unexpected trends were identified. The
Epson printer rollout continues, and although the incoming calls to the desk have not reduced as expected, this
is largely due to the ink related issue, currently being addressed with Epson. The majority of the incoming
printer calls are resolved at the desk, and Epson are providing additional consumables to cover the higher
usage until the problem is fully resolved.

There has been marginal improvement in the Engineering SLTs but 3 out of 4 are still below target. Discussions
are in hand to agree revised targets once all major improvement actions have been implemented.

A total of 80 complaints were received, 39 were passed to Fujitsu for information only. 6 were for investigation
and comment and of these, all were agreed to be justified. Specific corrective actions have been put in place
relating to communication of ETAs and engineer attendance in branch. The revised complaints process will be
implemented in June

2 Incident Overview

Summary: Within the Fujitsu Services domain for May 2007 there was 1 Major Incident.
There were 3 further service affecting incidents within the Fujitsu domain.

There were no incidents affecting VIP sites.

There were 2 further service affecting incidents outside of the Fujitsu
domain.

Major Incidents: Thursday 3 May. An IP stream failure occurred, due to a memory leak on
the LNS router “Hardy”. Branches switched to the other router “Laurel” and
caused an overload at a later time. 1921 branches were affected with loss of
on-line services from 09:10 to 10:00 and 593 affected from 10:00 to 13:30
when corrective actions removed Horizon connected branches from Laurel &
Hardy. Corrective action work, took place overnight on OCP 15813 to
upgrade the Memory in both routers. Call E-0705030120 and E-0704030248
refer.

Other incidents in Fujitsu Tuesday ha May. An IP stream failure occurred on delivery router “Smith”

Services domain: (there are 4 delivery routers) at 10:54, this had the potential to affect up to
25% of the branch estate. Service was restored at 11:08 by taking the router
“Smith” out of service and all branches were reconnected by 11:34 via the
remaining 3 delivery routers. The LD report was produced showing that 294
branches were affected by this incident. A faulty h/w card was replaced in
the router “Smith” overnight and monitored, and then re-introduced back into
the live service the next day. Call E-0705010399 refers.

Tuesday 15" May. Intermittent time outs on the A&L Link “B” to Wigan from
08:46 to 11:08. The incident was identified to a C&W circuit at Carlton Park
to Leicester router 2. The circuit was taken out of use for C&W and BT to
investigate. Traffic routed through Leicester 1 router. Investigations by BT
proved the fault to be at the local BT exchange, repaired and circuit brought
back into live service on Sunday 20". Calls E-07051501 19, 404 & 407 refer.

Monday 21* May. It was noticed that for 7, 1 minute periods from 09:00 to
10:44 that CAPO transactions were failing at a higher rate than normal on
both A & B links. Circuits checked with Network teams and EDS but no
explanation found. It was identified as a possible local network issue at
branch level but not confirmed. Not service affecting as retries working and
no calls to the HSD. We will continue to monitor for similar events on future
corresponding Monday's. Call E-0705210281 refers.

VIP sites affected this month: None

Incidents that were outside Tuesday 45” May. Degradation experienced on both A&L links to Bootle

the Fujitsu Domain: and Wigan starting at 14:10 and continuing intermittently until 16:04. All
retries of transactions working, no calls to the help desk. Conference calls
with POL and A&L. A&L investigated and identified the issue to be timeouts
on their mainframe Tandem to their firewall on the response to the request
back to the branches. A&L undertaking further investigations to resolve and
identify the root cause, as a firewall upgrade took place at the weekend. Call
E-0705150778 refers.

3 Business Continuity

3.1 Business Continuity Testing
On Saturday 5" May Fujitsu Services successfully completed the operational testing of the routers (test 5).

On Sunday e" May Fujitsu Services successfully completed the first phase of operational testing of the Data-
centre LAN (test 6).

On Sunday 43” May Fujitsu Services completed the second phase of operational testing of the Data-centre LAN,
the Catalyst fail-over test. This test was only a partial success due to a delay in Firewall failover in Bootle and no
connection for POLFS users. A retest of the failing element is planned for Sunday 10" of June.

During May Fujitsu Services successfully completed the Reference Data operational test.

On Sunday 20" of May Fujitsu Services successfully completed the operational testing of the Debit Card
Service/Electronic Top-up Services (test 25)

On the weekend of the 2" and 3” of June Fujitsu Services successfully completed the POLFS operational test,
failing-over from PLP in Bootle to QATest, the DR POLFS system, in Wigan.

3.2 Forthcoming Testing
A retest for the failing elements of the Catalyst fail-over is planned for Sunday 10" of June.
The Campus fail-over (test 2) is planned for the 15" to 18" of June.

The SMC operational test (test 12) has been rescheduled for the 1° of July to allow live operational changes to
be implemented.

3.3 Business Continuity Planning

During May further progress was made on producing the HNG-X Business Continuity Framework, the HNG-X
Operational Test Plan and initial work commenced on writing the HNG-X changes for the Business Continuity
Plans.

3.4 Business Continuity Incidents and Issues
No Major Business Continuity Incidents were raised during May.

Progress of the implementation and commissioning of the LST rig in the LEW02 Disaster Recovery site has been
delayed further due to network issues. A number of servers have required rebuilds. The LST commissioning
phase is expected to be completed during August.

The operational test for the loss of Bracknell 01, i.e., the relocation to LEW02, is therefore being postponed until
later in the year.

Commentary

Engineer Complaints

Despite the increase in SLT conformance, there was an increase in Engineering Complaints, including 7 around
online services. More detailed analysis is underway to identify the reason for the increase. The meeting was held
to review the end-to-end Incident lifecycle and target times set around specific points / hand-off in the process.
Data will be captured to track performance against these targets.

The Epson printer rollout programme to replace the Ithaca printers is underway and progress is continuing
according to schedule. To date 11371 new printers have been installed, which equates to 32.49% of the printers
in the estate. As this project continues the call, visit volumes and subsequently complaints volumes are all
forecasted to drop. The reduction in the number of counter printer faults and engineering visits will have a knock
on benefit on scheduling engineering visits. Analysis has been undertaken on the issue with printers printing
blank receipts, and Epson have identified a fault with the ink, which remains in their domain to resolve. Whilst
awaiting resolution the service desk continue to provide workaround and preventative maintenance instructions to
address this issue.

Printer Problems
Printer Complaints were normal for the month of May.

Online Services

The number of complaints against Online Services has decreased markedly in May and has returned to the
normal monthly figure. This is due to the completion of the IP Stream migration project and this decrease was
expected. GSM’s have been deployed in branches that experienced any migration issues. Work continues with
BT to improve the resolution process for physical phone line faults and internal faults within a branch.

5.2 Engineering Service
May 07 has seen call volumes again finish just under 6,000, with 642 A Priority calls in this period.

As the Epson roll-out continues to gather momentum, the number of engineering visits for the office counter
printers (Epson & Ithica combined) was at it's lowest for over 12 months. A trend we expect to continue as the
Ithica’s continue to be replaced by the more suitable Epsons.

From April the SLT figures have shown an improvement in all four measurable areas, The A priority calls fixed
within four hours rose from 73.0% to 74.8%, whilst A priorities resolved within six hours also improved to 86.8%
from 84.2%. Conformance on C priority next day calls showed final figures of 95.7%, an improvement of 2.5% on
the previous month which came in at 93.2%. Next Day +2 hours on C priorities reached 96.2% conformance after
being 94.1% in April.

These improvements have come as a result of added focus on the desk and service improvements discussed
and agreed being implemented and used on a day to day basis. There were one or two issues on the desk, but
measures have been put in place to continue to increase the speed in which calls are allocated to an engineer
once they reach the desk.

Base unit builds again prove to be the highest single point of failure when we attend A priority calls. A number of
improvements from the Service Improvement Plan have been implemented and further meetings have taken
place to look at reducing the overall leakage during a calls life.

We also suffered a higher number of Mobile Base Unit failures, some of these misses were caused due to poor
stock provision. This has now been addressed with spares better distributed.

The Auto SLA facility has also had a detrimental affect on service, and we have put a manual process in place
whilst this is resolved.

May has again seen all counter availability targets met, with all conformances in excess of 99%

5.3. Branch Network Services / Central Network

5.3.1. Branch Network Services
During May there were 1,342 calls logged across all Network Types into the HSD.

SLT's

The ‘Priority A - 6 hours’ SLT saw 27 failures from a total of 1040 closed calls measured, resulting in 97.4%
achievement. A contributing factor to the SLT conformance miss can be attributed to volume of incidents that
require investigation outside of the Incident Management process, thus requiring extensive investigation from
Fujitsu Services and Post Office Ltd.

Other issues in May
In previous months we have been reporting a higher then expect fall out rate from the IP Migration activity. This

activity has now almost completed with 10 Branches left to migrate. However there are still a number of
Branches to Migrate from ISDN to IP Stream. We expect a high volume of calls to be raised during this migration
activity as the sites remaining have experienced failure to migrate previously.

CMT have completed the move to “ownership” of a single incident, which has attributed to the increase in SLT
conformance. We expect further improvements over the coming months.

of the calls received at the Horizon Service Desk. The remainder of calls relate to the Branch Network Service.

7 Sense & Respond

Due to the timescales around production of the Sense & Respond data and analysis, the information in the SRB
will always be for the previous month. The full Sense & Respond report is issued separately.

The data captured during April has highlighted the top 2 highest demand types as being Hardware and Software.
Trend analysis has shown a decrease in total call volumes for both Hardware (-1277) and Software (-1275) from
March to April.

During April, the HSD fixed 62.6% of calls received (10254) and this was a slight decrease of 0.4% of total calls
from March.

For networking calls, a focus has been made to begin tracking ADSL and ISDN call volumes and the mean time
to clear. Trending has highlighted an increase in call volume from March to April for both ASDL (+140) and ISDN
(+369) calls. ADSL IPS calls are expected to increase due to the current roll out. Further investigation will
continue to identify why ISDN calls are increasing.

The communication stacks have now been renamed which will ensure more effective tracking of calls. Other
achievements include the production of a daily WIP report which will enable HSD & Service Management to
identify any peaks or issues within the team more efficiently. Additionally, the CMT has been restructured to
enhance call ownership across the team to ensure calls are followed up and closed more efficiently within the
SLA period.

Sense and Respond investigation has identified for Pin Pad errors that CAPO cards were used for the majority of
transactions before a fault was reported. It has been highlighted that the CAPO proportion is in line with the
transaction volumes and based purely on the numbers of transactions, we would expect 75% or more errors on
Pin pads to be reported in CAPO transactions.

For hardware demand, the highest call volume received is Counter Printer faults (approximately 3750 calls
received in April which has decreased from approximately 4000 calls in March). The Epson printer roll out
programme is presently a third of the way in progress. A rise in ‘Printer printing blank pages’ calls has been
identified and after investigation it seems the ink in the printers is drying out while the post office is shut for a few
days. This Ink was not used at the original pilot sites. The call rate for the two printers is now identical meaning
that currently the Epson’s are not having a positive impact on call volumes to the desk. Engineering calls for this
product however does remain very low as the desk can use a workaround to get the printers working again.

Another area of focus is regarding chase calls. 2720 repeat calls were logged in April of which 14.8% were
engineer ETA chase calls. Future projects include ‘Customer Expectations’ into the Call Coaching check Sheet
and to provide advice to PMs regarding not calling for ETAs on C priority calls until the SLA has passed.

An additional objective regarding engineering calls is to reduce the amount of calls to HSD from Touch, Fujitsu
and ROMEC engineers. This is to help increase agent availability for taking PM calls. CMT are trialling
‘recommendations’ using multiple ‘plus’ signs to break up the block text that Clientele receives at TOUCH. This
will be monitored to see if calls from engineers have reduced in the advice category as a result.

A project monitoring Inappropriate calls between the HSD and NBSC desks has achieved its objective of
reducing the number of calls between the desks from 10% to 5%. The team will continue to monitor the
inappropriate call volumes and ensure monthly communication is maintained between the HSD and NBSC desks.
