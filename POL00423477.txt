Whistleblowing shouldn’t threaten your career

Blowing the whistle should not be a life changing or career failing event.
People should be able to raise concerns in the workplace, knowing they are
taken seriously and will not be treated badly for doing the right thing.

Yet, when we hear the world “whistleblower” in the headlines, we associate
the word with career failure and being expelled from the workplace.

[ HYPERLINK "https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/uk-48984491" ], Katherine and her husband,
both boarding school teachers, spoke out against “systematic exam
malpractice” and said, "we both lost our jobs, it was absolutely horrendous...
«wl was completely clueless and didn't even know I was a whistle-blower",

Staff being confident to whistle blow should always be accepted as a
part of day to day good business practice

At Post Office, our culture of openness and accountability is vital to ensure
Post Office matters as much tomorrow as it does today.

That’s why we believe that noticing a problem, and speaking up about it
should be a part of business as usual.

Questioning other people’s behaviour or policies can feel disloyal, but to us,
it is the ultimate act of loyalty, as you’re raising concerns in the interest of
our people and our business.
