EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Introduction

1. This case is the final business and financial
statement for the Benefits Agency contribution to the
wider BA/POCL Programme. It builds on the BA/POCL
Development Group Feasibility Studies which were
accepted by Ministers in early 1994 and were the
basis on which the Treasury approved continuance of
the Programme in July 1994.

2. The endorsement for the Programme’s strategic and
business fit was obtained in December 1995. This
case is now submitted to seek financial approval for
the award of contract to the recommended supplier,
chosen in accordance with the Evaluation Board’s
findings.

3. The case outlines BA (including Social Security
Agency (NI) and War Pensions Agency) elements of the
BA/POCL Programme development and includes costs
which will be charged by suppliers. Although this
primarily deals with BA-related issues, the dual
responsibility for the project means that much of the
evaluation and implementation work will remain a
joint venture.

4. The BA/POCL Programme is being taken forward as a
Private Finance Initiative (PFI) in line with current
Government policy. The chosen supplier has
adequately satisfied the Programme that all the PFI
criteria have been met. This business case deals
with the approval of BA-related costs and a separate
HMT submission deals in more detail with the
rationale behind the PFI option.

Objectives

As partners in the joint programme, BA and POCL have
committed to the overall objectives for automating
benefit payment which has a high relevance to the
business of both organisations and is considered
important in combating the shortcomings identified
above. Additionally, the introduction of a new method
of payment, with the development of the CAPS
Programme, will contribute to the Agency’s business
vision and IS/IT strategy which aim to fundamentally
improve how services are delivered. These
developments are also consistent with the
Departmental strategic position.

The encashment of benefits at post offices will be
conducted by use of a card which once authenticated,
unlocks payment details held in a Payment
Authorisation System (PAS). Therefore the

value is held in the system and not in the card.

The Card Management System (CMS) which is to be
provided by the suppliers, will be responsible for
arranging the supply, personalisation and
distribution of the benefit payment card and
monitoring its status throughout its life.

The Payment Authorisation System (PAS) will receive
authorised payments from CAPS Customer Payment
Computer System (CPCS) . When the customer presents
their payment card at the post office counter PAS
will authorise any payments via the POCL Transaction
Management Service (TMS). Payment encashment will be
undertaken via the Benefit Encashment Service (BES) .

Costs and Benefits

The investment appraisal covers the costs and
benefits attributed to the introduction and operation
of the Benefit Payment Card and the BA share of the
costs associated with automating post offices. There
is a reduction in the cost of administration of
benefit payment and also a significant increase in
the prevention and detection level of benefit fraud
which the card system will achieve.

The Appraisal is over 10 years, with sunk costs in
financial years 1994/5 and 1995/6 and eight years of
the contract. The NPV over this period is £1036m
which includes additional fraud savings if the OBCS
option is included. The supplier’s charges have
reflected both with and without OBCS.

The business case reflects all known costs relating
to the BA’s responsibilities for administration of
the card system including changes and additions to
current processes relating to Instruments of Payment.
It also includes all aspects of service charges which
will be paid directly to the supplier (PAS and CMS)
or via POCL {BES) as well as the increased charges
relating to POCL services covered by a separate
commercial contract with BA for all payment
transactions undertaken by POCL on behalf of BA.

Some allowance has also been made for increased costs
due to possible future increased charges related to
change controls. The full cost of administration
over the IA period is £897m.

Risk and Dependencies

High level risks to the programme, including those
stemming from dependence on projects outwith the
Programme’s scope, have been identified and a system
for managing/eliminating those risks has been
introduced. In addition, sensitivities associated
with possible delays to delivery of the card payment
system or of CAPS, and volume changes over the life
of the contract have been assessed. Within the
majority of parameters tested the overall business
case remains affordable in terms of administrative
expenditure. The overall NPV remains strong when
programme savings are included.

Programme Control

A Committee and Management Structure is in place to
provide direction, exert appropriate control over all
elements within the programme and track progress. All
committees will be used fully and effectively to
ensure the delivery of a value for money service.

The programme will conduct a post implementation
review, particularly important in this instance due
to the ‘pathfinder’ status of the PFI elements.

Conclusion

The programme represents a major change to the
strategic use of IT in the Department. It provides a
fraud-free method of payment and at a healthy return
on investment through substantial gain in programme
savings. It also provides an improved single
database on customers and supports greater
efficiencies in the Department's accounting
processes.

1. INTRODUCTION

1.1 Benefits Agency (BA) together with Post Office
Counters Ltd. (POCL) has established a joint programme
to provide automated services to support the end-to-
end process of paying all of a person's social
security benefits administered by BA/SSA(NI)/WPA
through post offices - replacing order books and
girocheques by a new product based on a plastic card.

1.2 This business case has been prepared as the final
step in a process which culminates in approval to
sign contracts. References to BA should be taken to
include War Pensions Agency (WPA) and Social Security
Agency (Northern Ireland) (SSA (NI)) who are parties
in the project.

1.3 The BA/POCL Programme is going forward as a Private
Finance Initiative (PFI) in line with current
Government policy and following HMT guidelines. The
business case reflects the latest position on
commercial negotiation with both the chosen supplier
and POCL. Final PFI charges for the chosen supplier
and contractual issues with POCL have now been
finalised. All these charges have been incorporated
in the investment appraisal associated with this
business case. The order of rollout of benefits on
CAPS has been agreed with Minister and should only be
subject to minor changes. This case is submitted to
obtain Financial Approval for the Departmental
expenditure required by this project.

1.4 The case covers the period from August 1993 when the
joint Benefit Agency (BA) and Post Office Counters

2. BACKGROUND
BA/POCL Feasibility Study Report.

2.1 The current system of payment of Social Security

deficiencies, while maintaining customer choice.

A feasibility study report was produced [Strategy for
Benefit Payment at Post Offices February 1994] which
proposed that a new system should give:

(1) positive authorisation of benefit payments at

Risk

5.13 Transference of risk is a major consideration of the

is generally accepted that the private sector

Post Implementation Review

10.8 A Post Implementation Review is the evaluation of
the programme after it has been completed, or become
well established as a going concern.

10.9 The aim of the review is to improve project
appraisal, design, management and implementation. It
is intended to obtain the maximum benefit from the
accrued experience. A review of this programme is
likely to be particularly valuable in its examination
of the joint approach with POCL, the procurement
process and PFI.

10.10 The review should normally follow this sequence:

(1) define the objectives of the evaluation: i.e.
what aspects of these decisions and activities
are to be evaluated

(2) establish the objectives of the decisions and
activities which are to be evaluated

(3) consider what should be taken as the baseline
for comparison with outturn

(4) compare the outturn with the baseline and
explain any important differences and draw any
lessons which can be learnt for the future

(5) present the results

(6) communicate the results or recommendations.

10.11 This programme will carry out a review at the
appropriate time. In the mean time, two check-step
reviews have already been undertaken by external
consultants as well as an audit by a combined team
from BA and POCL Audit departments. The
recommendations of these studies have been taken into
account by the Programme.

POCL OBJECTIVES

The POCL objectives are summarised as follows:~

(a) to provide continued customer choice of services
available at post offices and be acceptable to
customers;

(b) to retain and strengthen POCL’s clear branding links
with its customers

(c) to maintain POCL’s customer base;

(d) to support government policy of a nation-wide network
of post offices;

(e) to be capable of introduction in all post offices;

(£) to retain and enhance POCL’s commercial and financial
integrity;

(g) to improve overall efficiency and cost effectiveness
for POCL and its clients;

(h) to support and help agents in the development of their
private business;

(i) to be acceptable to staff and agents;

{j) to facilitate automation of other areas of POCL
infrastructure, e.g. accounting and distribution, and
support wider business information needs;

(k) to retain and gain new business by improving quality
of service to all clients;

(1) to provide the flexibility to meet a diverse range of
existing and potential client needs and applications;

(m) to provide long term stability for the Post Office
network as a retail outlet for benefit payments;

(n) to adopt a flexible and efficient approach to IT
systems and adhere to industry standards so as to
secure the benefits of development in IT and retail
sectors, thereby achieving value for money and faster
delivery of new products and services;

(o) to retain customers’ trust in the integrity of POCL
and improve the quality of service to customers;

(p) to facilitate the provision of added value services,
e.g. card and/or token issue and management, which
will enhance POCL’s service offering;

(q) to ensure the migration of appropriate automated
systems without any reduction in service levels;
to be a key enabler in helping improve POCL’s
competitiveness, in meeting its business partners’
needs, and enhancing future business viability.

ANNEX SIX

BA/POCL BUSINESS CASE: SPREADSHEET ASSUMPTIONS

NOTE: Base year for calculations is 1995/1996; all costs
are at 1995/1996 prices.

PROJECT MANAGEMENT & SUPPORT

These costs cover BA Programme & Commercial Team staff and
non-staff costs. Staff costs include the Programme Support
Office, core project staff and consultancy support. Non-
staff costs include accommodation costs, Demonstrator
costs, DIFR costs, IT implementation support and running
costs. 64% of the total PM & Support costs are “sunk”
costs and the remaining 36% are attributable to PES years
1996/97, 1997/98 & 1998/99. There are no anticipated costs
in this category thereafter.

IMPLEMENTATION COSTS

Assumes that all generic CAPS training costs (e.g.
PDCS/CPCS) and any benefit-specific training costs (e.g.
IS/ChB/JSA) will be included in the CAPS Business Case.

It is assumed that CMS and other card-related training
material will be provided by the chosen supplier but
training will be delivered locally by BA/SSA District
Training Officers.

Assumed that 149 Training Officers will be required to
deliver 0.5 days training per person to BA District,
Central Services and ES staff. Provision has been made for
course preparation by Training Officers (1 day) and
monitoring effectiveness of training. In all, 60,202 staff
(excluding SSA) in grades LOI and LOII will require
training and it is assumed that each training officer will
deliver 14 training sessions, each session accommodating
30 staff. Volumes and unit costs have been updated using
1996/1997 Whole Unit Times (WUTs) and formulae provided by
Staff Resources Unit (SRU).

Costs will be incurred over a 3-year period, 1996/97
(25%), 1997/98 (50%) and 1998/99 (25%).

The cost of providing benefit-specific and generic CAPS

RUNNING COSTS - STAFF

Contract management

In consultation with POGAB it is estimated that the
additional costs of managing the new contracts with POCL
and the Supplier should not exceed 0.75% of the total
contract price (excluding VAT).

Additional Contract Related Costs

Some additional costs have been identified as a result of
the Supplier’s proposals eg consumables and CAPS interface
costs. These are estimated at £19.2m in steady state.

BA_& SSA Districts

Although some existing work processes will be discontinued
(e.g. action on receipt of returned IOPs), it is assumed
that there will be some new processes required directly
associated with Benefit Payment Cards (e.g. the issue of
temporary tokens). The additional processes identified
have been checked by Staff Resource Unit (SRU).

Admin Savi

With the introduction of Benefit Payment Cards some
existing work processes will be discontinued. Extensive
liaison with SRU has identified obsolete work processes
and using SRU volumes and WUTs, Admin Savings in BA/SSA
Districts have been calculated. Order Books will not be

girocheques will be required for customers who cannot be
paid by Card and for administrative purposes. The
following work processes will no longer be required and
savings have been calculated as follows:-

No__longer issue Order Books (production costs): the
savings have been calculated based on current Printed
Output Volume information supplied by ITSA. The
diminishing requirement year-on-year is based on the CAPS

Potential savings of £17,835,083 in OB production costs
have been identified due to a reduction in volumes but as
ITSA anticipate a need to increase printing activity to
provide information about payments made by card, only
£6,878,931 (38.5%) at steady-state is currently thought by
ITSA to be realisable.

Lost /Missing/Stolen Order Book action: it is estimated
that 477,193 OBs are reported as lost, missing or stolen
each year. At a unit cost of £21.65, calculated using SRU
formulae for staff, personnel and appraisal elements, this
represents annual savings of £9,295,466.

Returned OB Action: 17.28% of OBs issued are recalled.
Savings have been calculated taking account of the fact
that work in this area will be significantly reduced and
this represents steady-state savings of £8,320,533.

Postage: postage costs will reduce and savings have been
calculated on the basis of 1.5 books per envelope for
system-produced books; and 1 book per envelope for manual
issues. This represents steady-state savings of
£15,026,455.

No_longer issue Girocheques (production costs): as for
Order Books. The savings have been calculated using
Printed Output Volume information supplied by ITSA. The
diminishing requirement year-on-year is based on the CAPS
migration and Post Office roll-out assumptions.

Qther Contract Savings

POGAB’s contract negotiations with the GIROBANK have
identified an estimated saving of £53.4m in steady state
as the usage of giros for benefit payment diminishes. To

renegotiated Contract A. to give savings of £24.5m at the

Programme Savings
Order Books:

Data now available from ESNS on stop notice activity
appears to offer a more accurate basis for calculation of
volumes and values of order-books at risk. The national
‘at risk’ amount has been estimated at £187.7m. The
difference between volume/value of order-books on which a
stop notice is issued and the ones actually returned has
been used to calculate the volume/value of the total
amount at risk. It is further assumed that probably only
25% of this at risk amount is likely to be fraudulently
cashed. The benefit payment card is estimated to capture
90% of this likely at risk amount; £168.9 in steady state.
A fuller explanation of the methodology used to calculate
the ‘at risk’ amount is contained in the business case
submitted by IOP Security. The new methodology has been
approved by BA Resources.

The SAFFE exercise due to be undertaken shortly will help
to further refine the baseline for the encashment figure
of at riskfoils.

The OBCS option will bring forward the savings achievable
in the PES years but, has cost implications which are
under consideration.

Girocheques: as part of the reconciliation process, a
record is made of the value of Girocheques known to have
been fraudulently encashed. This data has been used to
calculate the anticipated saving from the prevention of
Girocheque fraud.

Programme Risks

Risk Unresolved CAPS Programme migration issues.

Background:

A number of imminent CAPS Programme migration issues require

resolution: .

® scope, timing, and length of go-live for all Benefits;

* the scope of the Operational Trial and

e transitional issues associated with registration and
payment of individual and multiple Benefits during
implementation.

Countermeasure:

* CAPS have scheduled first card payment to take place on 23
September with first payment by the renewal cycle planned
to start on 21 October 1996;

* General Matching Service is to undertake a scan to identify
the number of Child Benefit customers cashing benefit at
each Post Office, including identifying alternative payees
at the LGL offices;

* Migration issues are being resolved by the Programme
Authority and include SLAs as well as addressing the
requirements of individual benefits prior to uplifting onto
the system;

e The Component Release Strategy which will provide a
comprehensive view of both technical and business products
required for the whole CAPS system, has now been baselined;

e The Benefit Migration Template, which will provide a basis
for individual benefit uplifts onto the new system, is
currently being reviewed;

e The Business Implementation Strand have finalised the BA
District Rollout Strategy, and the District Rollout
Schedule is being Quality Reviewed;

e The CAPS Technical Implementation strand have agreed a
scoping paper and plans for initial card payment in
September.
