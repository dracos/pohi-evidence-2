7.4. Joint working documents also demonstrate Fujitsu’s and Post Office’s mutual understanding
regarding the existence of, and potential impacts of, BEDs. By way of illustration, the April
2012 ‘Reconciliation and Incident Management Joint Working Document’ identified the
prospect of a “system fault”, including a “sofiware bug (introduced through either design
or coding)” or “system crash” to “affect transactions”. Joint working is also evident in

the management of particular BEDs about which the Inquiry heard evidence in Phase 3.5°

7.5. The management of BEDs was also an important part of the release management process.
Mr Smith’s evidence was that the Release Authorisation Board would monitor BEDs within
the system and would, on occasion, ‘defer’ the fixing of particular identified issues to later
releases.® That is, Post Office would agree that a system update could be implemented,
notwithstanding that it was known that there were unresolved BEDs within the system.°’ Mr
Smith explained that Post Office employees would “have [deferred issues] on their radar
screen and decide whether they were worth following up or not.”® The types of issues
‘deferred’ in this way varied. However, some of the ‘deferred Peaks’ (for example, in
relation to release S80) were subsequently identified by Deloitte as giving rise to a “risk of

erroneous data which could result in sub-postmaster detriment”.
