(4) Giro receipts and declared cash/cash on hand receipts

(a) We have disclosed those giro receipts and declared cash/cash on hand receipts that we have.

(2) Balance snapshots

(a) It is not mandatory for a sub-postmaster to print, or if they do print, to retain, a balance snapshot
report. It is only used as a rough guide. Ms Oglesby states that she does not recall being given a

balance snapshot for every day by Mr Castleton. We have disclosed those balance snapshots
which we have received from the Post Office.

(b) For the detailed reasons previously provided, we do not believe that the balance snapshots will
really be useful in determining this dispute. You have not explained why you consider that they
will.

(c) The overriding objective is to deal with cases justly and “justly” includes saving expense and

dealing with a case which is proportionate to the amount of money involved. The Post Office’s
claim is for £27,115.83 plus interest and costs. We have already made clear to you that the costs
involved in this dispute will significantly exceed the value of the claim. This is largely because of
the time taken up dealing with Mr Castleton’s groundless allegations about “computer generated”
losses. As you have acknowledged, our client’s disclosure is already voluminous. You already
have the full audit trail in the transaction logs. We therefore do not propose to incur costs
debating balance snapshots. No discourtesy is intended.

(d) On 2 December 2005, the Post Office sent two managers to the Marine Drive branch to try to
obtain the information you had then requested. They were informed that the branch would not
release any data without them signing for it and that they should photocopy the documents. They
were also led to believe that all the accounts referring to past accounting years are in Mr
Castleton’s storage areas. When the managers tried to collect those documents, Mrs Castleton
refused to deliver them up. Accordingly, if you seek any further balance snapshots that Mr
Castleton claims to have printed, we suggest that you ask him to produce them.

(3) Software updates

We are instructed that software is updated remotely and that software update could result in a desktop
being closed and restarted. However, even then, this would not result in computer generated losses, as
you assert in your Part 18 Reply.

We enclose two Excel spreadsheets of all the updates for the period January to April 2004 for your
information. Software updates are relatively rare and (unless prior agreement is made with the sub-
postmaster) only tend to take place outside office hours and with occasional extensions at the weekends
(i.e. when the sub-postmaster is not using the machine).

In the product column of the attached spreadsheet, I = in store, D = delete, U = update. Counter one
shows that there were no updates between 8.30am and 5.30pm over the period. Counter two shows (with
the exception on 2 February) there were no updates between 8.58am and 10.06pm over the period in
question.

On 2 February there were 24 instances of IUD at 2.09pm and 1 instance at 2.10pm, which indicates a
process (lasting 1 minute) starting at 2.09pm and completing at 2.10pm. We are instructed that this is
due probably to a counter installation that would have been with the subpostmaster’s knowledge and
consent.
