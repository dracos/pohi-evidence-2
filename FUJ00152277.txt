1 MANAGEMENT SUMMARY

Within the Fujitsu Services domain for Oct 07 there were no Major Incidents and no service affecting incidents, 2
incidents relating to VIP sites and two further service-affecting incidents outside the Fujitsu domain. Details are
given in the Incident Overview below.

Branch and counter availability continue to remain above target at 99.8% and 99.4% respectively. Management
of Engineering Service levels remains a priority: A priority performance is steady, and C priority performance has
shown a good improvement over previous months. Weekly reviews of figures are in place to track the progress of
the pilot service against the critical success factors.

125 OBC changes were delivered in the month, with zero combined failures.

The revised complaints process has given Fujitsu better visibility of the issues received relating to the Horizon
service. However, there have been some problems with the consistency of the information passed from Post
Office. This is being addressed, so whilst the process is generally having the desired effect, the reported figures
are inconsistent.

There were 2 branches which failed the Day J targets, both of which were exacerbated by poor escalation. This
has been addressed with the resolving support teams and the desk.

Significant activities from the Service Improvement Plan are included in this review book. Further activities
implemented this month include additional improvement in the diagnosis and support process for comms calls,
and these have begun to deliver benefits in overall call resolution times. Other activities highlighted in this report
also focus on continuing improvements in service to Branches to minimise service interruptions.

5.2 Engineering Service

We have continued to see a drop in call volumes throughout October compared to September. Although the
overall call volume for the month shows an increase to 4425, the average number of calls attended per working
day has dropped to 177 from 184. There were a total of 704 A priority calls for this period.

October also saw the end of the Epson roll-out, barring a small number of mobile sites, and the number of calls
for combined counter printers dropped again from 834 to 673.

The biggest change within Engineering in October was the start of a new three month SLT trial. Whilst service for
C priority calls remains the same, the SLTs for A priority calls has changed, with the first target being that we
should have an engineer on site within three hours of the call arriving in engineering. The second target is for us
to have fixed the call within six hours of it reaching engineering for base unit calls and within four hours in total for
peripheral-related calls. The table below shows our conformance levels during the first month of the trial.

Meeting the 3 hour response was one of the key drivers for us to achieve all our targets. Re-education of the
Touch desk was required to help drive this change in mindset and we believe we have now reached the point
where they really understand what we are trying to achieve in terms of improving the PM’s experience when
requiring an engineer visit.

C priority conformance rose dramatically from 91.9% to 95.3% for the month, achieving the SLT target. Next day
+2 hours on C priorities also improved to 96.2% from 92.6% in September.

5.3. Branch Network Services / Central Network

5.3.1 Branch Network Services
During October there were 907 calls logged across all Network Types into the HSD.

SLTs

The ‘Priority A- 6 hours’ SLT saw 13 failures from a total of 907 closed calls measured, resulting in 98.6%
achievement. The majority of SLT failures have been attributed to delays in call handling at the service desks.
This is being taken up with the Service Desk.

Other Activities During October
The ongoing amendments to the fault resolution process are continuing to improve our ability to manage

incidents and find faster and more accurate resolutions. We are also working closely with engineering, and
Branch Network Services attended engineering training to reaffirm the process amendments.

VSAT - Since BT has replaced equipment at selected branches and made a significant change to the carrier
services in an attempt to reduce the transaction time outs, we have noticed an improvement on these failures.
We are now awaiting confirmation from POL to close this problem.

October saw a small decrease (-8) in the number of Network Calls received.

5.3.2 Central Network

The Access Layers for ADSL (IP Stream), ISDN (Metered) and VSAT branches remained extremely stable during
October. The last 50 branches dialling into the ISDN (FRIACO) network were successfully migrated to ISDN
(Metered) network in time for the product withdrawal. In addition the last 398 branches using the IPS Office
product will be migrated to IPS Home MAX by the end of November.

The Backbone WAN suffered a couple of unexpected circuit outages but the failover worked perfectly so there
was no impact and the affected circuits were later reintroduced out of hours. We are in the process of integrating
the new datacentres including Maidstone EDG2 and migrating support sites to HNG-X compatible WANs.

The Datacentre LANs were also stable with no major incidents.

7 Sense & Respond

The data captured during October has highlighted the top 2 highest demand types as being Hardware and
Software. Trend analysis has shown the total call volume for Hardware during October to be similar to September
(9884 calls) and a decrease for Software calls (-415) from September to October.

For Pin Pad errors, the total volume of Pin Pad calls for October was 1188 (equal to the volume received during
September) with the highest call type being ‘Smart Card reader faults’ (approx 540 calls).

HSD will be discussing the feasibility of adding new Repair codes so reporting can be made more useful and less
of a manual task. Furthermore, the HSD will be monitoring Pin Pad calls on a monthly basis and prompting the
Post Masters of these branches to use the card cleaners when logging calls.

HSD are to attend the next Hypercom service review meeting. This will be a good opportunity to revise frontline
fixes and has the potential to reduce engineering visits.

For hardware demand, the highest call volume received is for Counter Printer faults (4360 calls received in
October — a decrease of 635 calls from September). This represents a total call reduction of 804 calls over the
past 2 months for counter printer faults.

Site diaries have been introduced to selected pilot sites which offer a hints and tips section for branches. In
September, 19% of frontline fixes were resolved by turning the counter printer off/on. The roll out of the site diary
could potentially reduce a high number of counter printer calls received by the HSD.

HSD/Epson completed improvements to the HSD Epson knowledge base. This was a good opportunity to revise
frontline fixes and has the potential to reduce engineering visits. Epson have agreed to provide HSD with a
breakdown of Unicom printer failure analysis to help improve frontline/FMS diagnostics.

With the introduction of Triole for Service we are now able to split chase calls by type. The highest chase call type
in October was for Engineering Support and the highest volume of engineer cancellations was for C45 calls.

Inclusion of ‘Customer Expectations’ into new Call Coaching database has been completed and this will allow the
HSD agents to be assessed on the accuracy of setting the correct expectation with the customer. This could in
turn reduce the number of chase calls to the desk.

An additional objective regarding engineering calls is to reduce the amount of calls to the HSD from Touch,
Fujitsu and ROMEC engineers. The Call volume for the HSD has decreased slightly from September to October
by 219 calls (804 received) and CMT have also experienced a decrease of 266 calls (1429 calls received).

Within the monitor faults project, 1499 calls were logged during October as a monitor fault (an increase of 287
calls from September). This represented a total of 1140 faults (76%) being resolved by engineering and 359
faults (24%) by the HSD. It has been identified that 40% of engineer monitor faults were for gateway counters.

A project monitoring inappropriate calls between the HSD and NBSC desks has achieved its objective of reducing
the number of calls between the desks from 10% to 5%. The team will continue to monitor the inappropriate call
volumes and ensure monthly communication is maintained between the HSD and NBSC desks.

Software demand has been previously investigated in depth, especially around the areas of application/system
freezes and error messages. While software demand is fairly high at a top level, further drill down resulted in
many different issues and scenarios. Many of these issues are already known and documented, with the majority
having a workaround. The key areas of failure should be addressed in the forthcoming HNGx software. As a
result, it was felt that the Sense and Respond activities should focus on hardware, especially as the only way to
reduce most of the software demand would be to further develop the Horizon software which will soon be
replaced.
