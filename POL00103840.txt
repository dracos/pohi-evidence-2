All

Further to the ad hoc GLO Subcommittee meeting this week, we yesterday attended a
Conference with Sir David Calvert-Smith (a retired Court of Appeal judge and former
DPP) with Tom Cooper and Tim Parker to discuss Post Office's historic criminal
prosecutions, in particular in relation to the points raised and discussed concerning
oversight of the review. Post Office's criminal solicitor (Nick Vamos of Peters and
Peters) also attended.

The position arrived at by Tom and Tim, with our full support, was that:

e Post Office would retain Sir David to act in a capacity as special advisor to
the Board to assist it in its task of scrutinising and testing the criminal advice it
receives, including in relation to key strategic decisions that need to be made,
the approach to be taken on the disclosure review and issues relating to the
CCRC and the Court of Appeal. In addition, Sir David will instructed to consider
and approve Post Office's approach to the ongoing disclosure review. For
example, Sir David will not be involved in the review's day to day process but
will be asked to comment on and approve the disclosure review guidelines
which will specify the circumstances in which disclosures should and should not
be made. This would have obvious optical benefits including demonstrating
that the disclosure review processes has been independently overseen and
approved. In order to maintain a degree of separation from the criminal
lawyers, it is proposed that HSF will instruct Sir David on behalf of the Board.

e  Inaddition to Sir David's appointment, Post Office would also appoint a criminal
QC with extensive defence experience to work alongside Brian Altman QC. This
would complement Brian's prosecution experience and ensure Post Office is
able to demonstrate optically its commitment to consider the review from both
the prosecution and the defence perspective. Working alongside Brian would
ensure that there are no delays to the review timeframe, which is important
given the CCRC's indication that they are to take their recommendations to the
commissioners at the end of March, which could result in some referrals to the
Court of Appeal. The appointment of an additional criminal QC along with
oversight from Sir David, we serve to limit any public criticism of Post Office's
handling of the review. We are currently identifying suitable silks with sufficient
experience.

* Sir David impressed us in our meeting by showing a willingness to
challenge points made on all sides, and also an appreciation of the non-
legal (public perception) points. As such, we think he will be well suited
to the role the Board needs on these issues going forward.

*® Interms of the review going forward, the team are making good progress with
the review and we are looking to finalise dates for a detailed session on the
individual cases. It is envisaged that the precise parameters of the disclosure
exercised will be finalised after that session, which will give Sir David an
opportunity to get up to speed and allow us to follow the lead of the CCRC, if
and to the extent that any references are made in the second half of March.

1am obviously happy to discuss the above in more detail.
