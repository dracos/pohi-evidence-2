Post Office Limited —v- Castleton

Thank you for your faxed letters of 5 and 13 April. We apologise for the delay in reverting to you, caused in
part by the writer’s absence from the office last week: and then annual leave following the
Easter bank holiday.

As you'know, we recently had a conference with counsel and with our client to deal with settling Replies to
your client’s Part 18 Request for Further Information. As part of a general review of this claim, we also
considered the issue of mediation.

We have previously made clear that our client is happy to mediate this claim. We made clear at the outset
however our reservations regarding the usefulness ‘of a mediation before disclosure had taken place. We do
not propose to rehearse the reasons again here. Suffice it to say that we do not accept your client’s contention
that the balance snapshots are a “red herring”.

In any event, there are other categories of documentation that we believe we need to have sight of before a
meaningful mediation can take place. Foremost among them is the comprehensive audit trail for Marine: Drive
Post Office that we have requested previously. Weido not believe you have ever actually addressed the issue
of whether it is accepted that such a document exists nor whether your client will provide disclosure of it.

Your’ client’s position, as set out in your letter of 6 March, is that “Purely for the sake of commercial
expediency, the Post Office is willing in principle to consider a mediation to hear Mr Castleton’s proposals to
settle the outstanding indebtedness”. With the greatest of respect, this does appear to create a very clear
impression that your client has prejudged the outcome of the mediation and is seeking to set constraints on
what it is willing to discuss at a mediation,

1 :
Our client presently has not proposals to settle the alleged indebtedness. Given your client’s position, we
question whether any useful purpose would be served in incurring the costs of a mediation if there is no
realistic prospect of a compromise being reached. The fees of the mediator whom you have proposed are
£3,500 plus VAT, with the costs of the mediation venue to be added.

When you then add to that figure the parties’ respective costs of preparing for and attending the mediation, it

is apparent that the costs associated with the process will be quite considerable indeed. We do not think it

would be sensible to incur those costs when, as things stand, there appears to be little prospect of the parties
_ being able to mutually acceptable compromise.
