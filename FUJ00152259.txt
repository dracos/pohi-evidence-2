2 APRIL REVIEW OF SERVICE

2.1: MANAGEMENT SUMMARY

Fujitsu’s service for April 2006 maintained generally high levels of service, with all but 4 Service Levels being achieved.

The issue of increased hardware incidents noted in March has continued and ongoing investigations centre round the Ithaca Counter Printers. The MTBF
appears to have been severely affected predominantly by the wider introduction of the new Postal Orders. The volume of calls to the help desk for Pin
Pads continued to be high, with the majority being fixed without an engineer visit. The profile of these calls is being investigated to ensure that the root
cause is fully understood and addressed.

The OBC volume dropped substantially against March, and the trend is forecast to continue. There was a failure rate of 5.22% against the new OLA of
4% minus closures and actions are in hand to improve this measure against the falling volume.

The engineer service performance remained above 95% compliance with the exception of the Priority A calls and On-site repair time. The number of
engineer visits was 4782 against a 'target’ of 3875. The Priority Engineering service was implemented across the estate from 20" April as planned.
Feedback continues to be positive on this and the profile of incidents logged is being managed to ensure it remains in line with expectations.

The number of faults logged for On line service provision continues to drop. During April the number of faults resolved within 24 hours was slightly
lower at 84.4%, although the number of faults resolved within 48 hours was marginally improved.

There were no major incidents in April.

2.1.2 Availability and Problem Management

Summary: Within the Fujitsu Services domain for April 2006 there were no Major Incidents. There were a further 3 incidents effecting service within
the Fujitsu domain. There was I incident affecting a VIP site. There were 3 further service effecting incidents which were outside of the Fujitsu domain.

Major Incidents: There were no Major Incidents this month.
Other incidents in Fujitsu Services domain:

Wednesday 19" April. CAPO counter Time Outs. Failing I in every 40 from 09:00 to 09:44. Incident due to change applied to the Bootle Authorization
Agents under OCP 13303. OCP change regressed and all CAPO agents working to Wigan. Calls E-0604190123 & 171 refer.

Friday 21" April. An incident occurred with CAT I & 2 at Wigan from 13:44 to 13:56. This caused 256 retries in 5606 transactions connecting via
Wigan. Call E-0604210561 refers

Wednesday 26th April. A network incident occurred at 14:57 resulting in 28 branches losing On-line services. The outage was caused by the failure of
an STMI circuit between C&W and BT. Service was restored at 18:07. The fault proved to be on the BT SDH portion of the circuit. Service was restored
when BT reversed the jumpers on the circuit at their Birmingham exchange. Call E-0604260728 refers.

VIP sites affected this month.

VIP Site Sunderland 005343: During building relocation the ISDN service connection having been re-located to a new building a week ago, lost on-line
ISDN service connection Thursday morning. BT attended site Friday morning and resolved by 10:30.

Incidents Outside the Fujitsu Domain:

Monday 10" April. Link timeouts >19% was caused by a loss of service to the Co-op & Smile Bank from 10:03 to 11:03. Link confirmed issue and loss
of service.

Thursday 20" April. The A&L service to both Wigan & Bootle was lost from 10:57 to 11:28. An A&L technician was doing unplanned work on the
A&L firewall, which caused the break in service. Call E-0604200455 refers.

Friday 21" April. Link had an Incident with NATWEST from 09:58 to 10:46, causing 733 retries in 16,500 transactions. Call E-0604210265 refers.

2.1.3. OBC Branch Change Service Summary

April 2006 realised a total of 164 changes undertaken, this being a reduction of 96 changes compared to March, 10 of these were cancelled changes.
With the exclusion of ‘Closures’ 115 changes were undertaken. Fujitsu were responsible for 7 change requests not being completed on the target date.
For Fujitsu this represents a failure rate of 5.22% against the new 4% OLA minus Closures. It also represents a failure rate of 4.55% against the
original target of 5%. There were 2 line plant failures; this represents a failure rate of 3.90% against the old 4% target excluding Line Plant. There is a
difference of -1.84% compared to February’s Target without Closures. Compared to the February calculations a difference of -0.67% against the 5%

target rate and -0.84% against the 4% target rate, excluding line plant.

Post Office Ltd were responsible for 3 change requests not being completed on the target date, which represents 1.95% against the 4% target, which

shows an improvement against the February calculations of 2.74%.

Corrective Actions

April saw an increase in ISDN line faults at the point of installation. Fujitsu continues to work closely with Cable & Wireless to further identify root

causes and any possible trends in order to decrease these occurrences.
Fujitsu OBC has also committed to provided future feedback on actions taken at meetings in a more timely fashion.

2.1.9 Business Incident Management
There were 20 BIM incidents during April. This is 23 less than reported in March 2006.

Of these incidents, 1 was within the 8 hour response SLT relating to the ‘Network Banking State E23’ exception type.
19 BIM incidents were within the 5 day response SLT.

The reduction in the number of BIM incidents can predominantly be attributed to the S90 Counter upgrade, which has reduced the number of State 4
exception type incidents to zero this month.
2.1.10 Engineer Services — Site Visits By Engineer

In April the pilot of the Priority Service was completed and rolled out across the whole estate. There were 4782 visits in April, of which 3726 required a
unit exchange, and 1056 were resolved without needing a spare. The number of visits for counter printers was again high at 2109 following the
introduction of Postal Order printing; PIN PAD visits were also high at 844, though this is a reduction against March.

The excess printer calls and exchanges this month are attributable to the introduction of Postal Order printing. Investigations are ongoing with the printer
repairer to determine the extent to which operator technique and media suitability have affected call volumes. Meanwhile the spares loop has been
boosted with additional printers, and the capacity for doing repairs and replacements has been increased.

2.1.11 Security Services

Automated Key Refresh

An average of 27 branches a day were selected for key refresh while the key refresh algorithm was turned on for this month resulting in a total of 745
branches being targeted for refresh. The key refresh algorithm is now permanently turned on, and will be continually monitored.

Manual Key Refresh

There were no manual keys generated this month.

3.2.2 ONLINE NETWORK SERVICES

The network performance in April 2006 was good and the network banking authorisation times have remained consistent throughout this year. There
were no major incidents but several smaller incidents and external incidents that affected service to the end users. The improvements in the C&W
domain have continued to provide stability to the WAN infrastructure.

There were 1375 network related calls received at the HSD and the biggest group was 372 calls related to ADSL software errors requiring counter
reboots to restore the network service. It is expected that some of the software fixes included in the $92 release will reduce the number of assisted
reboots. The incident with CAPO also caused an increased online transaction call volume.

HSD branch incident routing improvements have delivered the lowest ever volume of C&W referrals that were returned with No Fault Found coding.
The MTBF and MTFR have remained stable across most networks with the exception of the high volume of unrelated PES failures at VSAT sites.

The BAS router software upgrade project was completed without incident and these routers now benefit from standardised IOS versions, configurations
and improved security levels. The networks team have now started planning a series of OCPs to bring the PIX firewalls up to a standard hardware and
software configuration.

4.3 KEY CROSS DOMAIN PROBLEM UPDATE

The new RAG status for the key cross domain problems are based on the criteria —

Business Impact

BI score

Root Cause RC score

High (H) >20 outlets affected Root cause unknown, no progress or no workaround in place

Medium (M) >1 <20 outlets affected Root cause determined, implementation agreed/needs to be agreed or
workaround in place

Low (L) <1 outlet affected Fix implemented and problem being monitored

RAG status = BI score x RC score = (RED = 6-9) (AMBER = 3-6) (GREEN = 1-2)
The differentiator to be applied between a RED & AMBER RAG status score of 6 depends on whether the RC is known and whether a workaround is in
place i.e. if a the score of 6 is derived by a “Root cause unknown, no progress or no workaround in place = RC score of 3” then the RAG will be red.

If the RC score was derived by “Root cause determined, implementation agreed/needs to be agreed or a workaround in place = RC score of 2 then the

RAG will be amber.
