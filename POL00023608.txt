GROUP LITIGATION FACTS
Audience: POL colleagues/intranet/ external as required

e Group Litigation is where a group of people with the same or similar grievances join
together to make a claim in the Courts against a person or organisation

e The litigation involving Post Office is part of a long running dispute between Post Office
and a group of mostly former postmasters who have made serious allegations about
technical, operational and contractual matters.

e Between 2012-15 Post Office performed independent investigations and engaged in
mediation for some individual complaints and in February 2016 it was reported that some
former postmasters involved had obtained funding for litigation against Post Office

e In April 2016, a High Court claim was issued against Post Office and in March 2017 the
Court issued a Group Litigation Order (GLO), a procedural tool to enable the Court to
manage litigation affecting multiple parties

e Asis usual practice in group litigation, the Group Litigation Order was advertised and
promoted for a time specified by the Court to enable potential claimants to seek to join
the claim. This was done by the claimants’ legal representatives between March -
November 2017. There are now 555 claimants, with individual complaints that span the
past two decades. They represent a very small proportion of people who have worked in
our network using our systems during that time

e The case is very complex and the Judge ordered that it will be heard as a series of trials.

e The first trial, which finished on 5 December 2018, was to establish the correct meaning
and interpretation of the contract between the Post Office and Postmasters — the
Common Issues trial. The Judgment from this trial was handed down (made public) on 15
March 2019.

e The principal finding in the Judgment is that the contract places a number of additional
obligations on Post Office which changes the way in which we work with postmasters

e Beyond the legal finding, the Judgment made strong criticisms of Post Office behaviours,
which we’ve taken extremely seriously. In recent years we’ve made huge improvements in

e The Judgment underlines that the way in which we help postmasters operate their Post

e Other key points handed down in the Judgment

e Both independent experts for the claimants and Post Office in the litigation confirmed that
the Horizon system is robust and compares well with comparable systems used by other
retail and financial services companies each day. Or if Judge rejects both experts
agreement re robustness: We remain confident that, with 47 million transactions a week
successfully processed at 11,500 Post Office branches, Horizon is a reliable system

e The Court’s findings do not affect the products and services we provide for our millions of
customers through our UK network of 11,500 Post Office branches

e Neither the Common Issues trial nor the Horizon trial have or will determine liability or the
individual claimants’ cases. Further hearings and trials regarding these matters are being
scheduled through into 2020.
