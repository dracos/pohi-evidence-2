A. Background

1.

In November 2013 the NFSP executive and its membership voted in favour of POL’s
‘Vision 2020” strategy and in particular: (i) changes to Network Transformation (e.g.
enhanced compensation and support, Transitional Locals, the “Cliff’); and (ii) a new
future funding model for the NFSP. The concept of Network Extension was discussed
and although the NFSP leadership expressed private support for this (e.g. they saw the
strategic need to respond to the competitive threats POL faces) formal agreement was
not reached (e.g. individually subpostmasters see Network Extension as cannibalising
revenue). It was expected that negotiations on Network Extension would take place
during 2014 alongside other discussions with POL.

Considerable progress has been made over the past 16 months in relation to Network
Transformation, in large part due to the enhanced compensation and support now
available. Progress has also been made with the Transitional Locals process which
appears to be going well. The NFSP’s “active support” (i.e. which it offered to POL in
November 2013) has primarily taken the form of non-resistance; there has been very
limited evidence of the NFSP getting involved on a network-wide basis or day-to-day.

3. POL has been in discussions with the NFSP since the 2013 agreement was reached,
primarily on business-as-usual matters. The nature of the dialogue has improved and
we have been reassured by the progress both parties have made in working
collaboratively. However recently POL’s discussions with the NFSP have become

4. This meeting is therefore a timely opportunity to: (i) remind the NFSP of Government's
support for POL and Network Transformation; (ii) remind the NFSP of the need for POL
to be commercial and respond to competitive threats it faces; and (iii) encourage the
NFSP to continue to work collaboratively with POL to deliver progress on all fronts (i.e.
not just the NFSP’s future funding model). Together these will help to protect the long-
term future of the post office network.

5. We expect that the NFSP will want to present themselves as keen to see progress and
they may highlight POL as the unreasonable party (e.g. they want to move forward in
relation to their future funding and aspects of Network Extension). However the
conditions the NFSP have proposed in these areas, and their lack of willingness to
engage more broadly, puts POL in a difficult position. It would simply not be appropriate
(or commercially rational) for POL to accept the NFSP’s current proposals.

B. The Grant Agreement

6. New subpostmasters are less likely to want to be members of the NFSP than existing
subpostmasters which means that over time, as the network churns, the NFSP’s
membership falls. This decline, which is accelerated by Network Transformation, raises
important questions for the NFSP — i.e. in relation to how the organisation will fund
itself in the future and also whether it will still be relevant in its current form. POL is
sympathetic to these challenges and as management see value from a body that
represents operators, it agreed in November 2013 to a memorandum of understanding
which set out parameters for discussions on: (i) what a future NFSP might look like and
the role(s) it might play; and (ii) based on (i) how such an organisation could be funded.

7. Discussions have been progressing slowly since November 2013. In July 2014 a draft
15-year Grant Agreement was agreed between the two parties’ negotiating teams which
formed the basis for a “new” NFSP (e.g. more akin to a trade body than a union-like
organisation) and set out a direct funding model whereby POL would provide up to
£1.5m in annual grant to support the NFSP’s operating costs plus up to an additional
£1.0m to fund specific services (e.g. training, resolution of service issues and business
development). The NFSP took this draft to its executive in the Summer of 2014.

8. Soon after it was taken to the NFSP executive the Grant Agreement was leaked; this
caused the NFSP to change its position, seeking greater comfort and concessions from
POL. It replied to POL with a revised Grant Agreement in August 2014 and negotiations
have been ongoing around this ever since. Recently they have reached an impasse
over one new aspect that has been introduced by the NFSP — a “termination payment”.

9. The NFSP has proposed a “termination payment” equivalent to 3-years funding (i.e. a
lump sum payment of c.£7-8m) in the event, for whatever reason, the Grant Agreement
is ended early. The NFSP has also sought to “water down” triggers for the agreement's
“termination clause”. While it is important that the NFSP has protections against the
relationship ending the proposed structure of this payment, and the new triggers, are
considered unreasonable by POL. For instance if the NFSP breaches the terms of the
contract (e.g. by publicly coming out against Network Transformation) and POL were to
terminate the agreement, it would have to make this payment. This is not a position
POL or ShEx can support — it is not commercial and it does not represent Value for
Money. A termination for breach of contract should not ideally be compensated.

10. POL has proposed keeping the existing triggers for the “termination clause” but adding
a “break clause” after 5-years when both parties can walk away from the agreement.
POL is also keen to push back on the concept of a “termination payment” although it
has been clear that it would not enter an agreement on the expectation it would be
terminated at any point, to give the NFSP comfort. This ensures both parties’ interests
are aligned while also giving them both a window to walk away in the future. In these
circumstances POL might be willing to make some form of payment to the NFSP to
ensure that its long-term viability is not put at risk, but this is subject to negotiation.

11. POL genuinely wants a long-term relationship with the NFSP and we know the Grant
Agreement represents the NFSP’s preferred course of action, although POL believes it
is currently playing “hardball” in its negotiating position. The agreement gives the NFSP
long term funding certainty and independence and the opportunity to strengthen its
capabilities to add more value to its members. POL is keen to see a more secure and
capable NFSP that can work alongside it collaboratively, to strengthen the network.

12. The NFSP are also in discussion with the CWU about a possible merger. While we do
not see the rationale in this (e.g. it does not give the NFSP the same security over its
funding or its independence, there are significant cultural differences between the two
and we do not believe the overall NFSP membership would view this favourably) we
cannot exclude this as a possible outcome if negotiations with POL continue to stall.

C. The September 2015 “Cliff”

14. POL's November 2013 agreement with the NFSP included the "Cliff" (i.e. the point in
September 2015 after which POL can amend the compensation and investment
available to subpostmasters and also withdraw or reduce Core Tier Payment). POL has
been doing considerable work in recent weeks on this and has sought to engage with
the NFSP. To date the NFSP has resisted engagement, seeking instead to prioritise
work on the draft Grant Agreement.

15. POL has documented support for the "Cliff" from 2013 and its plans today are aligned
to the parameters set out at that time (although the NFSP might challenge this view).
Therefore while we encourage POL to seek the NFSP's formal support for the "Cliff" the
lack of a joined-up approach should not necessarily stand in the way of progress. Also
even with dialogue it might not be possible for POL to secure the NFSP’s support.

16. We do not believe that the NFSP knows what POL is planning to do with the Cliff from
January 2016 (i.e. withdraw the Core Tier Payment) and that notice of this will be given
in June 2015. This means that when engagement does start the NFSP is likely to react
negatively; they will likely claim this is taking place earlier than discussed in 2013 —
which is untrue — and use it as an opportunity to try and extract more funding and
compensation from POL. It is very likely that the NFSP will push for more compensation
to be paid to subpostmasters converting to Main model branches.

17. Since most of these branches will not be materially worse off after converting (i.e. they
receive investment in their branch, a transition payment and their income is protected
for up to 3 years) there is no sound basis for additional compensation. It should be
noted that both i loperate these types of branch.

D. Network Extension

21. In October 2014 POL reached agreement with the NFSP around a limited Network
Extension pilot that saw c.150 Mini Post Offices open across the UK. In the run up to
this agreement the NFSP had tried to restrict the number of access points that could
open near existing branches, but POL resisted as this represented an important part of
the pilot. The agreement expired in January and for POL to take forward any more
pilots we expect it will try and seek the NFSP’s support (i.e. to avoid vocal opposition to
the openings and also withdrawal of the NFSP’s support for Network Transformation).

22. Network Extension is important to enable POL to respond to pressures in the mails and
bill payments markets where competitors have an edge in terms of technology, cost and
access. POL will want to undertake more piloting activity in the near future and it is
therefore important — for the future of the network, including for existing subpostmasters
— that POL has freedom take this forward. NFSP support is therefore important. The
NFSP leadership privately support the need for a more accessible network in urban
environments, but they have publicly spoken out against POL’s plans.

23. The timing and shape of future piloting is still to be confirmed but we do expect some
activity to take place in the Summer when an early version of the new technology
solution will be ready and that this will include a wider range of mails services than is
available at Mini Post Offices. Other services will not be added until early Autumn (e.g.
bill payment, cash withdrawals and foreign exchange). POL is also taking forward
discussions with retailers which might host future pilots — these are ongoing and
importantly have included the idea of “whole estate” deals which the NFSP do not
support. POL will ideally want to have NFSP support before any further activity takes
place but to date they have refused to engage (i.e. focus on future funding
discussions).

26. Transitional Locals: Since July POL has been making good progress with this process;

or completed with 390 of these. Not all of these branches have expressed a preferred
course of action yet although 138 have volunteered to leave the network and 98 will be
submitting a financial assessment. In November 2013 the NFSP indicated they would
support implementation of the Transitional Locals process but to date they are not
known to have given any active support to POL. This is unfortunate.

27. Government Services: ShEx is continuing to work with colleagues across Whitehall and
at POL to make progress in relation to specific Government Services opportunities;
near term progress is most likely with HM Passport Office (e.g. in-branch renewals) and
Cabinet Office (e.g. online ID Assurance pilots). These are commercially confidential
discussions and it would not be appropriate to discuss them in detail with the NFSP.

28. Business Transformation: The NFSP has been historically critical of POL’s central
costs and may raise these in your meeting. POL is taking actions to address this and
management is targeting cost savings approaching £100m a year by the end of
2015/16. This is an ambitious target and it should have the NFSP’s support.

29. Subpostmaster Remuneration: The NFSP may raise ongoing concerns about falling
subpostmaster incomes (e.g. DVLA renewal in 2012, withdrawal of road tax discs).
This reflects the intensely competitive marketplace POL operates in, which underlines
the importance of Network Transformation in moving branches into sustainable retailers
and for POL to respond to the threats it faces in the most appropriate way.
