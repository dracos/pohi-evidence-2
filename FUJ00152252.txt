2 DECEMBER REVIEW OF SERVICE

2.1 MANAGEMENT SUMMARY

The service delivered by Fujitsu for Post Office during December 2005 can be characterised by both excellent performance in terms of Service Level
Target achievement with the notable achievement of no major incidents, but impacted post 22nd December by engineering service failures.

Overall incident volume from the PostMasters remained on par with previous months, taking into consideration the Christmas and New Year break.
However, it should be noted that the online service network related incidents logged with Fujitsu was at a 12 month low with almost half the volume
logged just 3 months prior in September. This is a result of the proactive incident management and process improvements which have been deployed. In
addition, the average transaction time of just 0.34 seconds across ADSL connected branches.

The Service Level achievement across all services has been comparable to the excellent previous months. However, between 22nd December and 13th
January, the visiting engineering service has suffered following a transition of spares logistics suppliers. The root cause was a failure in the automated
ordering systems between Fujitsu and a third party which resulted in a manual process being implemented to order spares. The knock on effect meant an
increased Work In Progress stack. During this time, Fujitsu and Post Office worked together to prioritise engineering visits and minimise impact to
trading. At the time of writing (14th January), this service has been restored back to business as usual, but the effect to B priority service level,
complaints and remedial penalties will be seen during December and January.

2.1.3 OBC Branch Change Service Summary

December 2005 was very quiet as a result of the change freeze due to the Post Office’s busiest period of the year. Nevertheless December remained a
very successful month. A total of 104 OBC changes were received, no changes were cancelled. Fujitsu were responsible for 3.5 change requests not
being completed on the target date. For Fujitsu this represents a failure rate of 3.37% against the 5% target rate. As there was I line plant failure this

rate and 1.10% against the 4% target rate. Post Office were responsible for 5.5 change requests not being completed on the target date, which represents
5.29% against the 4% target, which is a difference against the November calculations of -4.12%. There were 3 joint failures.

2.1.9 Business Incident Management

There were 65 BIM incidents raised during December, 9 more than November.

Of these incidents raised 46 were within the 8 hour response SLT (predominantly relating to the ‘Network Banking State 4’ exception state) and 19 were
within the 5 day response SLT.

2.1.10 Engineer Services — Site Visits By Engineer

There were 4764 break-fix site visits completed in December, compared to 5344 in November, of which 3693 resulted in unit exchanges. and the overall
performance of the engineering service continued to be delivered around our SLT. However, in mid-December, Fujitsu Services underwent a change in
Logistics supplier, and some system errors and spares management issues resulted from this. The change impacted ability to replenish some engineers’
bootstock, and to get certain spares to some engincers in time to complete calls within SLA. This in turn has impacted the Average Downtime/Time to
Repair figure because it took engineers longer to complete some calls.

Service Improvements

FJS are constantly working to deliver improved Break-Fix SLTs and will continue to give senior management focus to the current problems in getting
stock to engineers.

In January, the new mobility box and power supply unit for the mobile counters is being introduced. This should deliver increased reliability of the
mobile counter. FJS are working on a programme aimed at reducing the numbers of engineering visits required; FJS have also proposed a number of
initiatives in which Post Office Ltd could assist in this area.

Remedies

In December there were 335 remedial calls , which is higher than in November (142 ). Penalties incurred £56,502. Conformance was again maintained
across A priority Service Level Targets, however Local and Intermediate B priority performance were not achieved, due to problems experienced within
Fujitsu Services over the Logistics transition from 19th December.

3.2.2 ONLINE NETWORK SERVICES
The network performance in December 2005 was absolutely the best month ever achieved since the introduction of online transactions.
e¢ No major incidents in the Fujitsu/C&W domain

Process improvements such as the scripting of standard changes to BAS routers (using input verification to reduce human error) and further
restrictions to the engineers who are allowed to perform unscripted changes have been implemented. The change freeze across all platforms has
also enabled postmasters to work uninterrupted throughout the important festive trading period. In addition C&W has confirmed an ongoing
change freeze will be applied to their MPLS network during working hours throughout the year.

Fujitsu are working with C&W on consolidating 2005 improvements to create an ongoing ITIL Availability Plan for their broadband network to
maintain this performance level. In early 2006 we expect to introduce further scripting and improvements to the monitoring on the NetCool
toolset.

e Fastest transaction times ever recorded with 0.34s average network banking authorisation time at ADSL branches.

Network packet loss throughout the Fujitsu/C&W network has been almost eliminated through CSIP project to replace all the CE, PE, LAR and
LCR routers. This work was completed over several months without any related incidents. In addition several software improvements have been
released that optimise transaction processing. Authorisation times have also improved for fixed/dialled ISDN connections.

e Lowest number of online services incidents exceeding 24hrs (also lowest helpdesk network call volumes for several years)
The online services daily report has shown that improved call-routing alongside additional testing (such as the VC/VP check) has successfully
reduced the volume of incidents that take an excessive time to resolve. Unfortunately BT caused several incidents by applying cease instructions
from organisations that are totally unrelated to the Horizon service

e¢ BT Enhanced Care trial at 1,000 branches for 2 months completed

As part of the ongoing work with Post Office in exploring network availability improvements we upgraded the BT service at 1,000 large ADSL
branches to Enhanced Care. The total BT charge of the trial was covered by Fujitsu at £8.65 per branch month (£17,300 over the 2 months).

During the trial 5 incidents were reported to BT at these sites of which 2 were fixed within the SLA, 2 were resolved on non-broadband
equipment and one was passed back to Fujitsu for referral to Post Office. We hope this trial has been useful in understanding the ROI of this
service.

4.3 KEY CROSS DOMAIN PROBLEM UPDATE

The new RAG status for the key cross domain problems are based on the criteria —

Business Impact Bl score Root Cause RC score

High (H) >20 outlets affected 3 Root cause unknown, no progress or no workaround in place 3

Medium (M) >1 <20 outlets affected 2 Root cause determined, implementation agreed/needs to be agreed or 2
workaround in place

Low (L) <1 outlet affected 1 Fix implemented and problem being monitored 1

RAG status = BI score x RC score = (RED = 6-9) (AMBER = 3-6) (GREEN = 1-2)

The differentiator to be applied between a RED & AMBER RAG status score of 6 depends on whether the RC is known and whether a workaround is in
place i.e. if a the score of 6 is derived by a “Root cause unknown, no progress or no workaround in place = RC score of 3” then the RAG will be red.

If the RC score was derived by “Root cause determined, implementation agreed/needs to be agreed or a workaround in place = RC score of 2 then the
RAG will be amber.
