All,

This is just to update you on a conversation David Cavender and I had this afternoon about the
scope of the recusal appeal.

We could broaden the appeal to say that the entire Common Issues Judgment should be struck
down on the grounds of bias. Arguably, as Green QC picked up, the logic of the second limb of our
application (that the Judge was gratuitously hostile) is that the Common Issues Judgment itself was
the product of bias (as well as prejudging the outcome of future trials, and thereby creating a risk of
bias prospectively). We therefore could, as part of the recusal appeal, attack the Common Issues
Judgment itself.

We do not think that is a good idea. It might well give the Court of Appeal pause if they thought
allowing the appeal would necessitate rerunning the Common Issues trial.

To deal with that problem, we could use the recusal appeal to attack the judgment on bias, whilst
saying that the findings on contractual formation and notice could remain untouched. The difficulty
is that there is no principled reason to leave some findings in place, whilst getting rid of the others.
The advantage of attacking the Common Issues judgment in this way is that it gives us another route
to get rid of the Judge's findings (in addition to the main appeal). If we do not dislodge the Judge's
findings, there is a risk that the recusal application succeeds, but on a basis which still leaves
certain damaging findings in place for the new Judge to be bound by/ rely upon.

However, on balance, we do not think it is a good idea to broaden the appeal in this way. The main
goal must be to get the Judge off the case, and anything that distracts from that is not worthwhile.
We did, though, want to flag this risk - that by not attacking the findings themselves on the ground of
bias they could outlive Fraser J's participation in this case.

If I have missed anything out, no doubt David will say so.
