IMPLEMENTATION “RISKS”

Barrie and I have discussed the levels of assurance and the areas of interest
which will provide greater demonstrated confidence in each others support
plans. I know this is fully supported by yourself and John as part of an
integrated approach to implementation planning. Many of the issues will be
addressed at the next Joint Implementation Meeting on 27 May.

There are however some associated risk areas which relate more directly to
the current review of release plans and carry implications for the start of
National rollout. I also want to raise them at the JIM but both you and Barrie
may wish to consider their implications in advance.

The issues are;

¢ The current plans envisage we will provide go live dates for the first batch
of rollout offices before we have entered live trial. As a result, the
associated programme of User Awareness and Training in the preceding 12
“weeks carries a significant level of risk by being defined before we have -
seen the release in live operation.’::: ) >"

- ¢ The process by which the rollout programme will ramp up to full

installation rates is currently restricted to a period of 4 weeks. Clearly the
confidence in supplier capacity (from both a Pathway and POCL
perspective) is something we have now jointly agreed to review, but the
way in which full capacity is reached, both in terms of timescale and
location is sufficiently critical to justify rexamination.

¢ The means by which the lessons learned from live trial are factored back
into the rollout procedures has not been clearly described and we also need
to review this process.

e We learned from release 1C there was a period of fault fixing which
extended for 4-8 weeks after the release date. Given the greater complexity
of release 2, there may be advantages in planning for a “fault fix” release
ahead of rollout to ensure the procedures are fully stable, have been tested
and have been factored into any associated alterations to training content
and user guides.

You will recognise, as I do, that the ramifications of these issues extend
outwith just the implementation cycle but it is important to get them onto the
planning agenda as soon as possible and the forthcoming JIM is a sensible
first step.

Following next weeks meeting I will discuss with Andrew Simpkins how we
take them forward within the review process but this is clearly a matter of
urgency to support the release planning activity now underway. Please let me
know if you have any concerns with this approach otherwise I would expect
Barrie and I to take them forward as proposed. .
