1 MANAGEMENT SUMMARY

Within the Fujitsu Services domain for May 08 there were 2 Major Incidents and 4 service-affecting incidents, 1
incident relating to a VIP site and no further service-affecting incidents outside the Fujitsu domain, detailed in
the incident overview below. The 2 Major incidents were isolated, with the first affecting 525 branches losing
web based services, and the second affecting all branches for a period of 7 minutes where all network banking
was lost due to an issue with the EMC Storage array. This second incident resulted in 50,000 transactions
timing out. Major incident reports have been produced for both incidents and have been fully resolved.

Branch and counter availability continue to remain above target at 99.81% and 99.62% respectively. The
Engineering Service continues to improve with all response time targets now being achieved.

The Pin Pad refresh button introduced in February has seen 260 incidents per month resolved remotely by the
data refresh button, a majority of which would have previously required an engineering visit.

1 offsite build was completed for Withywood with the assistance of Bristol Galleries Crown Office, allowing early
recommencement of services to the affected branch, that otherwise would have to wait for a PSTN fault to be
resolved by BT.

113 Preventative maintenance visits have taken place to date, with 69% of sites not logging a subsequent
hardware call. There have been no Back Office printer calls logged for any of the sites. The expectation is that
these will reduce the number of incidents and provide an enhanced service to the Post Office.

314 OBC changes were delivered in the month, with no failures.

1 non critical and 1 critical incident failed the Post Shops SLT, these have been discussed with Geller and some
corrective actions put in place, detailed in the Post Shops Summary.

The overall number of complaints logged with NBSC has decreased in May to 55, the lowest number for some
considerable time, with only 2 considered to be justified. Some additional graphs have been added to this
Service Review Book to show trends and associated activities to improve in this area.

No branches failed the Day J targets.

Significant activities from the Service Improvement Plan are included in this review book. A number of the
branch improvements have been in pilot and demonstrated positive results, and these will be rolled out in
agreement with POL over the coming weeks.

2 Incident Overview

Summary: Within the Fujitsu Services domain for May 2008, there were 2 Major Incidents.
There were 4 further service affecting incidents within the Fujitsu domain.

There was 1 incident affecting a VIP site.
There was no service affecting incidents outside of the Fujitsu domain

Major Incidents: Thursday 15th May. There were approximately 525 branches affected with a loss
of Web based services (PAF, DVLA & MoneyGram) from 09:35 to 11:45. The
incident was due to a VPN server (12) requiring a key reset which resolved the
issue. A MI report was completed by Mike Woolgar. Call 264400 refers.

Friday 23rd May. An EMC disc array incident occurred, due to a disc adaptor in a
“hung” state. This caused issues with access to the NPS for NWB transactions
and for the APOP service. Major Incident occurred at 9:02 and the NWB service
was unavailable from 09:05 until 09:12 due to no access to the NPS. (This would
have affected up 50,000 NWB transactions at this time). EMC remotely isolated
the disc adaptor to use secondary back up discs. EMC engineer attended site at
18:00 to replace at the SCSI chips in the disk adaptor, completed successfully. MI
report was completed by Mike Stewart. Call 278757 refers.

Other incidents in Fujitsu Wednesday 7th May. VSAT network suffered a failure affecting 27 branches from
Services domain: 16:35 to 16:50. Incident was investigated by BT into the root cause. The problem
was due to interference on the EB3 SAT Carrier Frequency. Call 252083 refers.

Monday 19th May. There was an IP stream failure from 09:30 to 10:00 affecting
up to 741 branches. Investigations showed that tunnels at BT central were
dropped and then re-established (for approx 2 minutes). Calls 269048 & 269747
refer.

Wednesday 21st May. The NPS1 (Network Banking Persistent Store) at Bootle,
suffered an ORA-07445 instance which caused the NPS to reload. This affected
up 750 transaction timeouts whilst the agents re-pointed to NPS2. Investigations
by Oracle show the crash was due to a memory corruption within the shared pool.
This is a possible one off incident, and Oracle advice is that increasing the shared
pool from 200MB to 500MB may help. The UNIX team is evaluating the Oracle
response. Call 274780 refers.

Tuesday 27th May. An IP Stream failure occurred at 11:55 to 12:30 affecting up to
332 branches. This incident was due to a BT power outage affecting
exchanges/branches in the Manchester/Cheshire area. Call 282737 refers.

VIP sites affected this Wednesday 28th May. A VIP Site Shrewsbury 153641 (10 counter) branch was

month: down at 10:02. A re-boot was unsuccessful. Engineer at site successfully re-
booted the Gateway but no counters worked. All counters required a re-boot,
counters 1 & 2 were up available from 15:00, and a total of 6 counters available
by 17:00. PM left to re-boot the remaining 4 counters as counters taking up to 40
minutes to reload. Engineer checked with branch in morning and Node 3 still
unavailable, attended site to replace counter node 3. Counter node 3 available
from 15:30. Calls 284344 & 286810 refer.

5.2 Engineering Service

Call volumes during May dropped to the lowest level for three years at 3793. There were 588 A priority calls
during this period.

The continued focus on A priority calls has resulted in an excellent SLT performance. All base unit SLT
measures were conformant along with two out of the four peripheral SLT measures. Only one additional passed
call would have resulted in conformance across all measures. The successful A priority SLT trial has resulted in
the measure being contractually approved, and performance can now be tracked in the table near the end of
this document.

We continued to deliver an enhanced reasonable endeavour same day service to WH Smith and VIP branches.
During May, 40% of all the C priority calls logged by these branches were attended the same day.

An off site build was performed for Withywood PO (341511) with help of Bristol Galleries Crown PO (020511)

The proactive preventative maintenance programme in May saw 16 visits being completed, limited only by the
lack of engineer whitespace. A call volume review on the 27" May demonstrated that the visits continue to be
successful as 69% of branches have not required an engineer since our visit. From 113 proactive visits, 22
peripherals were replaced with the main replaced component being the keyboard. Of these 22, three keyboards
and one Pin Pad were at single counter offices and would potentially have been A priority calls. Based on four A
priority and 18 C priority calls we have potentially saved POL approx 300 counter trading hours.

We hosted a Fujitsu stand at the NFSP conference in Torquay. Our presence, gifts, and hints and tips were
warmly received. It also gave us a chance to seek feedback from the branches on the end to end service, and to
enrol a number of branches onto a trial we intend on implementing later this year.

Branch Network Service / Central Network

5.2.1. Branch Network Service

During May 1575 calls were logged across all Network types, an increase of 67 calls from April. This increase is
attributed to various weather related Multiple Service Outages in the north of the country and in Turin (which is
the VSAT base station).

In total 30 calls failed to hit the 4 hour priority SLA. This gave 98% SLT conformance against the 95% target.

After the completion of the IPMax upgrade, a small number of sites were identified as having intermittent online
service issues, and were subsequently re-graded to a lower bandwidth rate. We are pleased to report that since
the sites were re-graded they have been running successfully with no communication issues. Core and the
Communications Management Team have been working together closely to ensure that the sites faced minimal
disruption during this migration.

The combined efforts of Branch Network services, POL and the Communications Management Team have
again seen a reduction in the average GSM bill. New scripts and new reporting are allowing both POL and
Fujitsu Services to efficiently and effectively monitor GSM usage and monitor the devices both out in the estate
and in engineering holding.

During May the Fujitsu Branch Network Service team attended the National Federation of Sub Post Masters
Conference in Torquay. We were able to seek feedback from end users and convert this feedback into valuable
Service Improvement Plans: for example some PMs felt they were not updated regularly enough; a CSIP has
now been put in place that updates should be given at least every three working days on long term issues.

The Branch Network Services Troubleshooting Guide has been accepted by all parties and is in the process of
being released to the helpdesk and engineers.

5.2.2 Central Network

This month there were several incidents on the Central Network Service detailed in the above Incident
Overview.

The interference on the VSAT carrier frequency (between the EuroBird3 satellite and the Turin base station)
was caused by parties outside the control of Fujitsu. In the past year the SkyPark operators have been
occasionally tweaking the frequency timeslots under change control to minimise the chance of interference.

Extensive effort was put into investigating why one of the BT Central tunnels dropped out. The incident affected
multiple Fujitsu customers who then needed to reconnect for online services. This particular BT Central usually
maintains a total of 4 unique tunnels to 2 different Fujitsu LNS. During this incident 3 of these tunnels were not
affected including 1 tunnel between the same BT Central and LNS over the same physical interface. There were
no capacity related issues occurring at the time.

5.5 Data Centre Operations Service / Data File Transfer

The only issue during May actually commenced during the last few days of April and is covered to some degree
under reconciliation issues below. However as it continued through to 19” May, it has therefore been reported in
this SRB.

On Monday 28" April, the debit card EMIS file received by RMGA from Streamline was incorrect due to a
Streamline Coding Error. As Streamline has a rigid process of ‘No changes at end of month’, they were unable
to provide corrected data until the 7” May. As this was outside of the system time period for which data is
retained and processed, it caused a large build up of outstanding data (146,016 transactions), that once
Streamline had provided such, there was a requirement for Fujitsu RMGA SSC to develop and run scripts to
‘manage away’ the discrepancies. As this task was a large overhead upon RMGA resources, it did not complete
until May 19th. TFS Call 236554 refers.

5.5.1 Day J Failures

There were no Day J failures during May.

5.6 Reconciliation Service

50 reconciliation incidents were cleared during May 2008. This is 82 less than that reported in April 2008. The
reduction is due to the fact that I was on annual leave for 10 days and therefore no BIMS were raised with
regards to Branch Closures.

There were 2 priority incidents cleared within the 8 hour response SLT.
The remaining 48 incidents, of which 46 were cleared within the 5 day SLT.

Reconciliation issues in May 2008

There was an issue with the EMIS file from Streamline delivered on 26" April where it contained a corrupt file so
Fujitsu were unable to process it. The file was eventually resent and processed on 19" May 2008.

5.7 Reference Data & Message Broadcast Service
No issues to report.

7 Sense & Respond

The data captured during May showed that the two highest demand types were being Hardware and Software.
Trend analysis has shown the total call volume for Hardware during May to be 6894 and Software calls to be
2159.

A total volume of 1243 Pin Pad calls were received during May, a decrease of 151 calls from April. HSD frontline
have been advised to check Tivoli events for all Pin Pad calls received to avoid any unnecessary HSD
diagnostics/engineer visits. Since the introduction of the data refresh button in February, approximately 260 calls
per month have been resolved with a data refresh. This represents 21% of Pin Pad calls, the majority of which
would have previously required an engineer visit.

2345 Counter Printer calls were received in May. 60% were resolved by a cleaning cycle or by changing or
adjusting the consumables. The planned IVR message would suggest these remedies, meaning that these calls
would not come through to the desk.

During May, 4092 chase calls were received, with the highest chase call types being for Engineering Updates
(1674) and Customer Calls (1522 calls). The completion of the Engineer PDA rollout should mean that more
accurate ETAs are provided to PMs, and the Comms Management Team will also update branches with long
standing online service issues more regularly. These measures should prevent some of these calls.

In regards to the project focusing on base unit calls, ‘Unit replacements’ are continuing to be the highest repair
code for calls, followed by ‘user advised / no action taken.’ The call volume from February to May has reduced,
but overall performance has been stable.

911 calls were logged during May as a monitor fault. This represented a total of 674 faults (74%) being resolved
by engineering and 237 faults (26%) by the HSD. A ‘Sharing Knowledge’ meeting between Engineering and the
monitor manufacturer has been planned.

After tracking Inappropriate calls data again throughout May, the calls received at the HSD totalled at 1450, a
slight decrease on April. This represents 10% of all calls.

HSD has highlighted all the sites that have made 3 or more Inappropriate calls for the month of April and passed
this information direct to POL. Outgoing coaching calls to be provided by HSD for repeat offenders. The aim is
to identify any outstanding issues at those Post Offices and to also coach the PM's to understand the difference
between the Horizon and NBSC desks. An article in Ops Focus is to highlight the differences between the two
desks.
