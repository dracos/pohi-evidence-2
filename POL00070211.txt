Based on the charge and additional claims against the property, as the Post Office obtained a
charging order following the making of the bankruptcy, I realised there was extensive negative
equity based on the agent’s valuation. I invited both Mr and Mrs Castleton to acquire my
interest, however, they chose not to proceed and I therefore concluded it was not cost effective
to take any further action.

Pursuant to Section 283A of the Insolvency Act 1986 the Property was re-vested in the bankrupt
by way of a certificate lodged at the Land Registry. I therefore no longer have an interest in the
property.

Bank accounts

The bankrupt disclosed a joint bank account with HSBC which held a minimal balance at the date
of the bankruptcy order. As HSBC had an outstanding liability they applied set off against this
credit balance.

During my review of the bankrupt’s affairs I located an additional account jointly held at the
Royal Bank of Scotland (“RBS”) which had not been disclosed. I undertook a thorough review of
this account but all funds had been spent by the date of the bankruptcy order so no realisations
were made.

Investigation

Prior to the bankruptcy order the bankrupt and his wife re-mortgaged the Property with RBS in
2005 and again with Commercial First in December 2006, resulting in a surplus of approximately
£42,000 paid into an RBS bank account. The bank account and additional records were
scrutinised following credit of this sum to determine how it was utilised and the majority was
paid for personal expenditure including school fees and it was felt this could not be recovered
due to the nature of the payees. It was clear that none of this sum was used to pay existing
creditors and the funds were used towards daily living expenditure and were not traced to other
bank accounts.

The bankrupt and his wife jointly owned two endowment policies, with Standard Life and
Scottish Provident, these were surrendered in February 2007 and the money paid into a joint
HSBC bank account. Following a review of the account certain payments to an associate of the
bankrupt was discovered, I wrote to this individual, however, following correspondence from
their solicitor, my solicitor advised me that the likelihood of recovery was small and it would be
uneconomical to pursue the individual further.

Following these investigations into the bankrupt’s affairs no other assets were discovered.
Distributions

No dividend will be paid to creditors as there are insufficient funds

Trustee’s Remuneration

At a meeting of creditors held on 15 August 2007, a resolution was passed that my remuneration
be fixed by reference to time properly given in attending to matters arising in the bankruptcy.

To date my staff and I have spent time of 102.9 hours at a total cost of £16,099, an analysis of
which is attached. No remuneration has been drawn all of this time has been written off. I
anticipate that no fees will be drawn in future in relation to this bankruptcy.
